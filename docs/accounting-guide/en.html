Standards and rules

Every company in Uzbekistan keeps its books in soums on one national chart of accounts. From 2027 new national standards based on IFRS for SMEs take over step by step.

Standards

The rules

From NSBU to NSFO in one timeline

Accounting follows Law ZRU-404 on Accounting of 2016. A company with foreign capital uses the same standards, chart of accounts and deadlines as any local company.

1 Jan 2025
New NSBU and formsNew standards on presentation and currency, a new chart of accounts and new statement forms
17 Dec 2025
IFRS by statusUnder PP-282 full IFRS are mandatory only for public-interest entities
2026
Last year on NSBUStatements for 2026 still follow the national standards NSBU
1 Jan 2027
NSFO No. 1 and No. 2National standards based on IFRS for SMEs and a new conceptual framework. NSBU 1 ends
1 Mar 2028
First NSFO statementsAnnual statements for 2027 are the first ones prepared under NSFO

What stays in 2027

  • All NSBU other than NSBU 1 apply until new standards replace them
  • The national chart of accounts stays. A new one is planned, not adopted
  • Books in soums, signed statements and the same deadlines

What to watch

  • Further NSFO on inventories, fixed assets, revenue and leases are in preparation
  • A new law on accounting and financial reporting exists only as a draft
  • TIFC participants may follow special rules since 25 July 2026
Common mistake

Expecting a whole new rulebook

Only NSFO No. 1 and No. 2 are adopted. Fixed assets, inventories and revenue stay on NSBU for now.

Good to know

Transition rules are still pending

A standard on first-time adoption is planned. We follow each new NSFO and adjust your plan.

Every month

  • Revalue currency balances at the Central Bank rate on the last day
  • Reconcile e-invoices and check their risk rating
  • Count the cash
  • Check that documents carry authorised signatures

Every year

  • Count inventories yearly and fixed assets every two years
  • Test the audit thresholds and the public-interest criteria
  • Reconcile the group package with the statutory books
  • Keep the year's documents for at least five years
Books in soums only

Currency revalued every month

Transactions at the Central Bank rate of the day. Exchange differences go to finance income or expenses.

Common mistake

Surprising the group with FX

Monthly revaluation moves the result. A dollar capital contribution stays at its contribution-day rate.

People and documents

Who keeps the books, and on what

The director organises the accounting and picks one of four models the law allows. Documents may be electronic, and every sale gets an e-invoice.

Four ways to organise

Own team led by a chief accountant
Accountant under a contract
Outsourcing to a specialised firm
The director keeps the books
The work moves, the responsibility does not. The company and its director stay answerable to the state. The provider answers under the contract
  • No certificate is needed. Where an audit is mandatory, the chief accountant needs higher education, experience and yearly training. This covers the outsourcer's staff too
  • The director approves two lists of signatories. Without their signatures a document is invalid

From document to archive

  • Primary documentsOn paper or electronic, like the ledgers and the statements
  • E-invoice for every saleIssued through an operator. A till receipt replaces it
  • Risk ratingSince 2026 each e-invoice is rated. High risk delays the VAT credit
  • Cashless above 400 BCVOnline till and a single QR code in retail
  • ArchiveAt least five years after the reporting year
Common mistake

One firm for books and audit

The auditor may not check a period it kept the books for. Use two firms.
