The first post-independence census counted 39,047,321 residents, about 810,000 more than the official estimate. Uzbeks make up 89.4% of the population, Tajiks 3.3% and Karakalpaks 2.2%. These are preliminary results.

## Geography and climate

Uzbekistan is one of only two doubly landlocked countries in the world: all its neighbours are landlocked too, so freight to an ocean port crosses at least two borders. Most of the territory is flat desert and steppe, including the Kyzylkum. People and farming concentrate in irrigated valleys, above all the Fergana Valley and the oases along the Amu Darya and Syr Darya. The east rises into the Tian Shan and Pamir-Alay foothills.

The climate is sharply continental, with long, hot, dry summers and short but cold winters. Spring and autumn are the best seasons for business travel.

## Languages

Uzbek is the state language, written in the Latin script, although Cyrillic is still common. Russian is widely used in Tashkent and in business. Karakalpak is an official language in Karakalpakstan. English is spreading among younger professionals and in banking and IT, but official procedures run in Uzbek or Russian, and employment contracts with foreign staff are normally drawn up in one of them.

## Public holidays

The Labour Code (Art. 208) sets nine non-working public holidays. Seven have fixed dates.

:::table Non-working public holidays
| Holiday | Date |
|---|---|
| New Year | 1 January |
| International Women's Day | 8 March |
| Navruz | 21 March |
| Day of Remembrance and Honour | 9 May |
| Independence Day | 1 September |
| Teachers' and Mentors' Day | 1 October |
| Constitution Day | 8 December |
| Ramadan Hayit (Eid al-Fitr) | First day; lunar calendar |
| Qurbon Hayit (Eid al-Adha) | First day; lunar calendar |
:::

The two Hayits follow the lunar calendar and move about 11 days earlier each year. Their dates are fixed by decree shortly before each holiday; the 2027 dates have not yet been published. In 2026 they fell on 20 March and 27 May.

A holiday falling on a weekend moves to the next working day. Each December a presidential decree also sets bridge days for the coming year; for 2026 (UP-257 of 24 December 2025) these were 2 January, 28 and 29 May and 31 August. Because the calendar moves every year, check the annual decree before fixing deadlines or closing dates.

## Business hours and working week

The Labour Code (Art. 182) limits normal working time to 40 hours a week, on either a five-day or a six-day week. Most offices and government bodies work Monday to Friday, roughly 9:00 to 18:00 with a lunch break around 13:00; factories and shops often work Saturdays too. Work on a public holiday is paid at least double, or with a day off plus single pay (Labour Code, Art. 263).

:::box Practical tip
- Avoid scheduling signings or filings around Navruz, the two Hayits and Independence Day, when extended breaks are common.
- The Hayit dates are confirmed only days in advance; build slack into spring timetables.
:::
