## Key indicators

Uzbekistan's GDP reached UZS 1,849.7 trillion in 2025, or about USD 147.1 billion, with real growth of 7.7%. Services contributed 3.9 percentage points of that growth, industry 1.7 points and construction 1.0 point. Growth accelerated in 2026: real GDP rose 8.3% in January to September, and the IMF's October 2026 World Economic Outlook raised its full-year forecast to 7.8%.

Inflation is falling but remains above the central bank's 5% target. Consumer prices rose 7.3% in 2025 and 6.5% in the year to October 2026. The Central Bank of Uzbekistan held its policy rate at 14% on 4 November 2026 and expects to reach the target by the end of 2027.

The two investment figures measure different things. The central bank's USD 4.4 billion is net foreign direct investment under the IMF's balance of payments methodology, after repatriation. The government's USD 43.1 billion counts all foreign investment and loans used in the year, of which it classes USD 38.2 billion as direct investment. Foreign direct investment and loans financed 65.9% of fixed capital investment in 2025.

Statistics for 2022 to 2025 are being revised to reflect the 2026 population and agricultural census, so some figures above may change.

## Foreign trade

Foreign trade turnover rose 20.7% in 2025 to USD 81.2 billion. Exports grew 24.0% to USD 33.8 billion and imports 18.5% to USD 47.4 billion, leaving a deficit of USD 13.5 billion. These are preliminary figures; revised data published in 2026 put turnover at USD 82.8 billion and exports at USD 34.2 billion. Uzbekistan traded with 210 countries.

China supplies almost a third of imports (31.2%), followed by Russia (18.3%) and Kazakhstan (7.2%). Russia is the largest export market. Machinery and transport equipment made up 33.8% of imports.

### Export structure

Gold dominates. Non-monetary gold exports rose to USD 9.9 billion in 2025, or 29.3% of all exports. Services, mostly tourism and transport, brought in USD 9.8 billion (28.9%). The other main export goods were:

- fruit and vegetables, USD 2.1 billion (up 36.9%);
- textiles, USD 2.6 billion (down 8.2%), over half of it finished products;
- non-ferrous metals, mainly copper, USD 1.4 billion;
- inorganic chemicals and fertilisers, USD 1.7 billion together.

Gold makes export totals volatile. In January to September 2026, exports fell 5.5% to USD 25.8 billion as gold sales dropped, but exports of goods excluding gold rose 31.6% to USD 13.3 billion.

> Exports of goods excluding gold rose 31.6% in the first nine months of 2026.

## Sovereign credit ratings

All three major agencies rate Uzbekistan two notches below investment grade. Moody's upgraded the sovereign in June 2026, and Fitch moved its outlook to positive the same month.

## Investment climate and guarantees

### Law on Investments and Investment Activities

The main act is the Law on Investments and Investment Activities (No. ZRU-598 of 25 December 2019), last amended in July 2026. It applies to domestic and foreign investors alike. An enterprise with foreign investment is one in which foreign investors hold at least 15% of the shares or charter capital. A new edition approved by the Senate in January 2025 would lower this threshold to 10%, but it has not entered into force, and the 2019 law remains the text in force.

The law gives foreign investors these guarantees:

- Stability: if later legislation worsens investment conditions, the law in force on the date of investment applies to the foreign investor for 10 years (Art. 19). The investor may opt into favourable changes.
- Free transfer of funds into and out of Uzbekistan after taxes (Art. 17).
- Protection against nationalisation and requisition, except in declared emergencies and with compensation (Art. 21).
- Non-interference by state bodies in lawful activity (Art. 15).
- Dispute resolution by negotiation, mediation, Uzbek courts, or international arbitration where a treaty or contract provides for it (Art. 63).

Land plots are leased to foreign-invested enterprises for up to 25 years (Art. 54).

### Investment agreements

Foreign investors can sign an investment agreement with the Government (Arts. 40 to 46). Such an agreement can add state guarantees, help with project financing, a special tax and payment regime and state monitoring of the project (Art. 22). Since 2026, however, individual tax incentives may be granted only by presidential decision, cutting a tax rate by no more than 50% for no more than three years (Law ZRU-1108 of 25 December 2025).

### Special regimes

Beyond the general law, two regimes matter most to foreign investors:

- Special economic zones (Law No. ZRU-604 of 17 February 2020): property and land tax exemptions for 3 to 10 years depending on the amount invested, customs relief on equipment and inputs, and a requirement that at least 90% of staff be Uzbek citizens.
- Foreign-invested enterprises with at least 33% foreign capital (15% for joint-stock companies): exemption from land tax and property tax for a period set by the President (Tax Code, Arts. 471–472, as amended by Law ZRU-1108 from 1 January 2026).

Each regime has conditions, such as reinvesting at least half of the tax saved, and benefits are clawed back if they are breached.

The 10-year tax stabilisation that Decree UP-4434 of 10 April 2012 gave new enterprises with a foreign cash contribution of at least USD 5 million lapsed on 1 October 2020 (Decree UP-6011). The general 10-year guarantee in Art. 19 of the investment law remains.

### Treaties

Uzbekistan has been a party to the ICSID Convention since 1995 and has 50 bilateral investment treaties in force; treaties signed with Italy and Jordan in 2025 are not yet in force. Most give access to international arbitration. Investors should structure holdings through a treaty country where treaty protection matters.

At 1 January 2026, 18,164 enterprises with foreign capital were operating in Uzbekistan.

## WTO accession

Uzbekistan is not yet a member of the World Trade Organization. Its working party was set up in 1994, and talks have accelerated sharply in recent years. By July 2026, 31 bilateral market access agreements had been deposited with the WTO, the latest with India. Uzbekistan brought 190 legal acts into line with WTO rules between March and July 2026.

The government had aimed to join in 2026. After the working party chair's visit to Tashkent in early November 2026, which prepared the 14th working party meeting, the target moved to 2027. Uzbekistan also holds observer status in the Eurasian Economic Union.

:::box What accession means for investors
- Bound tariffs and fewer discretionary import rules.
- Gradual removal of WTO-incompatible incentives; export-related profit tax benefits were already abolished from 1 January 2025.
- Stronger rules on technical standards, sanitary controls and state-owned enterprises.
:::

## Sectors with opportunities

### Energy and renewables

The state is replacing ageing gas-fired capacity and inviting private investment through competitive tenders and public-private partnerships. Solar and wind output roughly doubled in 2025, and installed solar capacity reached 5.9 GW by the end of the year, out of 10.0 GW of renewables (IRENA). Battery storage and grid upgrades are the next priorities.

### Mining and metals

Uzbekistan is a major gold producer and has large copper, uranium and silver reserves. Gold and non-ferrous metals together accounted for about a third of exports in 2025. The state is opening exploration licences, processing and downstream metal products to private and foreign investors.

### Textiles

Uzbekistan grows its own cotton, and policy favours processing it at home rather than exporting fibre. Textile exports of USD 2.6 billion fell 8.2% in 2025, and yarn still made up 27.0% of them. Clothing exports, however, rose 18.4% to USD 1.0 billion, and higher-value garments are where investment is going.

### Agriculture and food processing

Agricultural output grew 4.4% in 2025. Fruit and vegetable exports reached 2.16 million tonnes worth USD 2.1 billion. Cold storage, processing, packaging and greenhouse production offer the best returns.

### IT and outsourcing

Exports of telecommunications, computer and information services reached USD 921.5 million in 2025. IT Park residents enjoy a special tax regime and simplified hiring of foreign staff, which has attracted many foreign software and outsourcing companies.

### Tourism

Travel was the largest service export in 2025, worth USD 4.9 billion. Uzbekistan received 11.7 million foreign visitors in 2025 and 11.6 million in the first ten months of 2026. Hotels, resort development and services along the Silk Road cities need investment.

### Logistics

Transport services exports reached USD 3.2 billion in 2025. As a doubly landlocked country, Uzbekistan is investing in rail corridors to China, South Asia and the Caspian, logistics centres and air cargo.
