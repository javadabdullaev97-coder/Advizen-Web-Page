## Forms of presence

Foreign investors can incorporate a local legal entity or operate directly as a foreign company. The limited liability company (LLC) is the default vehicle for operating businesses and joint ventures. The joint-stock company (JSC) is used where the law requires it, for example for banks, or where the investor plans to raise capital from the public. A foreign company that only needs a liaison presence can accredit a representative office.

### Limited liability company

The LLC is governed by a new Law No. ZRU-1137 "On Limited Liability Companies" of 21 April 2026, in force since 22 July 2026. It replaced the 2001 law. An LLC may have one or up to 50 participants (Art. 8). A single-member LLC cannot itself be owned by another single-member company, unless that sole owner is a joint-stock company with one shareholder.

The new law sets out fiduciary duties for directors and supervisory board members, defines majority and minority participants and allows minority committees. A participant may still withdraw without the consent of the others and must be paid the actual value of its share within one year. Ownership of a participation interest now passes only when the change is entered in the Unified State Register. Participants may sign a corporate (shareholders') agreement under Article 358-1 of the Civil Code and must notify the company within 15 days of signing.

### Joint-stock company

A JSC issues shares and may have one or more shareholders. It normally has a supervisory board, which makes it heavier to run than an LLC; where there are fewer than 30 holders of voting shares, the general meeting may perform the board's functions (Law on Joint-Stock Companies, Art. 74). Banking and certain other financial activities can only be carried out through a JSC.

### Representative office, branch and permanent establishment

A representative office is not a legal entity and may not trade. It represents and protects the interests of its head office, typically through marketing and liaison work; the only statutory exception is foreign airlines. If it engages in commercial activity, its accreditation can be withdrawn.

Uzbek law does not prohibit branches of foreign companies, but there is no procedure to register them, and in practice registration is refused. A foreign company that carries on business through a fixed place in Uzbekistan instead creates a permanent establishment (PE) for tax purposes (Tax Code, Art. 36). A PE is not a legal form. The foreign company must register it with the tax authority within 183 days of starting activity, or within 30 days where the contract runs for more than 183 days (Tax Code, Art. 130).

> Uzbek law does not prohibit branches of foreign companies, but there is no procedure to register them.

### Individual entrepreneur and self-employed

An individual may trade as an individual entrepreneur without forming a legal entity and, since 1 January 2026, may use a business name. Self-employment is a lighter status for over 100 listed activities, of which no more than three may be carried on at once. Registration as self-employed is a notification through the Soliq mobile application or the taxpayer's personal cabinet, with no state fee. A foreign national can register as an individual entrepreneur only with a document confirming a place of residence in Uzbekistan.

:::table Main forms of presence compared
| Form | Legal entity | May trade | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| LLC | Yes | Yes | Operating company, joint venture |
| JSC | Yes | Yes | Banks, insurers, listed companies |
| Representative office | No | No | Liaison and marketing |
| Permanent establishment | No | Yes, for tax purposes | Projects and service contracts |
| Individual entrepreneur | No | Yes | Small resident businesses |
| Self-employed | No | Yes, listed activities | Freelance services |
:::

## Minimum capital

There is no general minimum charter capital for LLCs or JSCs. The requirement was abolished in 2019, and the new LLC law confirms that a minimum can only come from licensing rules for a particular activity (Art. 15). LLC participants must pay in their contributions within the period set in the founding documents and no later than one year after registration.

Sector rules impose their own floors. The minimum charter capital of a bank is UZS 500 billion. Insurers, securities market participants and exchanges have separate minimums set by their regulators.

An investor who wants the status of an enterprise with foreign investments should check two thresholds. The Law on Investments requires a foreign share of at least 15%. Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 66, Annex 11 (as amended by Resolution No. 165 of 28 March 2024), adds a charter capital of at least UZS 400 million, or UZS 200 million in Karakalpakstan and Khorezm region. Registration as an enterprise with foreign investments is refused if the threshold is not met (para. 40). Investors relying on this status should satisfy both rules.

## State registration step by step

Legal entities are registered on a one-stop basis by the Public Services Centres, under Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 66 of 9 February 2017. Filing can be made in person or online through the Unified Portal of Interactive State Services (my.gov.uz and birdarcha.uz) or through us-uz.gov.uz (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 393). One application registers the company with the justice, tax and statistics authorities at the same time.

- Choose the name and secure a legal address, usually by a lease or a letter from the owner of the premises.
- Prepare the charter and, where there are several founders, the foundation agreement in Uzbek.
- Obtain notarised and apostilled (or legalised) copies of the foreign founder's documents, with a notarised translation.
- Obtain a personal identification number (PINFL) for a foreign director. It is issued in one business day.
- A foreign founder signs the application with its own electronic digital signature (Resolution No. 66, para. 13).
- File the application and pay the state duty of 1 base calculation value (BCV, ~USD 37), reduced by 10% when filed through the Unified Portal (Law on State Duty, Art. 22¹).
- Receive the registration certificate and taxpayer number. By law, registration of a complete application takes no more than 30 minutes (Resolution No. 66, Annex 3, para. 2); in practice, allow one to three working days.

From 1 January 2026, Presidential Decree No. UP-214 of 14 November 2025 introduced the "Business in 15 minutes" principle. At registration the founder can order, in one step, an electronic digital signature key, a bank account, a licence or notification, registration of a commercial lease, a VAT certificate, an online cash register and the appointment of the director in the Unified National Labour System. Each service is optional and can be paid for with a single QR code.

:::box Practical tip
- Start the apostille or legalisation of the parent's documents first: it is usually the longest step.
- Documents must be signed, sealed and notarised exactly as required, or the centre will reject them.
:::

### Beneficial owners

Uzbekistan has no public register of beneficial owners and no fixed ownership threshold for disclosure. The registration application includes a questionnaire on the company's beneficial owners, and banks identify them again when they open the account. Foreign founders should have a chart showing the chain of ownership up to the individuals who control the company.

## Accreditation of representative offices

Representative offices are accredited by the Ministry of Investment, Industry and Trade under Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 76 of 7 February 2024, which replaced Resolution No. 410 of 2000. Applications are filed through a Public Services Centre or my.gov.uz. The head office submits its charter, proof of registration, a power of attorney for the head of office and the regulation on the office. No lease is needed for first accreditation; a copy of the lease is filed only on renewal. Foreign documents must be apostilled or legalised and submitted within six months of issue.

The Ministry decides within 5 working days. After approval the state duty must be paid within one month, and the certificate is issued within one working day of payment (para. 16). The office comes into existence on the day its accreditation certificate is issued. Accreditation runs for one to three years at the applicant's choice (para. 15). Registration with the tax authority is automatic (para. 22), and the office provides information on its activity at the Ministry's request (para. 25).

An office may accredit up to five foreign employees, who may make up no more than 40% of its staff. Accredited foreign staff need no separate work permit. Uzbek staff are employed by the foreign head office under Uzbek labour law.

## Legal address

A company's legal address is the address in its charter, and a representative office's is the address stated at accreditation. A private residence cannot be used. The tax authorities check that the entity is actually present at that address. If no one representing the entity is found there, the tax authority can suspend operations on its bank accounts (Tax Code, Art. 111). The address also determines the competent court and tax office.

## Opening bank accounts

Uzbek legal entities and accredited representative offices may hold accounts in soums and in foreign currency with Uzbek banks. Payments between residents inside Uzbekistan are generally made in soums, while cross-border transactions may be in any currency. Resident companies may open accounts with foreign banks only where a decision of the President or the Government, or an international agreement, allows it. Under Decree UP-214 the account application can be filed together with state registration. Remote opening is available to resident founders; where the founders are foreign, only the application is filed at registration and the bank completes its own checks (Resolution No. 66, para. 19).

## Licences and permits

Law No. ZRU-701 of 14 July 2021 sets three tracks: licences, permitting documents and notifications. All applications go through the License system (license.gov.uz) or the Unified Portal and are signed with a digital signature. The authority must decide on a licence or permit within 20 working days. If it misses the deadline, the applicant may start operating once it has paid the fee through the License system (Art. 28, parts 5–6). Since 14 August 2026, Article 28¹, added by Law ZRU-1164, lets a company start activities designated by the Cabinet of Ministers by notifying the authority before the licence is issued.

A licence may be refused only on the five grounds in Article 29: incomplete documents, non-compliance with licensing requirements, a one-year ban on obtaining a licence, false or distorted information, or a well-founded negative conclusion of a required study or inspection. Since 1 March 2026 foreign companies apply through their registered permanent establishment (Law ZRU-1099), and fines for licensing breaches are imposed on the organisation only, not on its officials. Notification-track activities can start as soon as the notification is accepted. Decree UP-214 added an optional preliminary assessment of an application in the License system, taking up to 20 days. Branches and representative offices of a licensed legal entity do not need their own licence; their addresses are added to the parent's licence.

## Typical timeline

:::table From decision to operating company
| Step | Typical time |
|---|---|
| Legalisation or apostille of parent documents | Varies by home country |
| PINFL for a foreign director | 1 business day |
| State registration of an LLC | Up to 30 minutes by law; 1–3 working days in practice |
| Bank account, digital signature, VAT certificate | At registration, if ordered |
| Payment of charter capital | Within 1 year of registration |
| Licence or permit, where required | Up to 20 working days |
| Representative office, as an alternative | 5 working days plus 1 working day for the certificate |
:::
