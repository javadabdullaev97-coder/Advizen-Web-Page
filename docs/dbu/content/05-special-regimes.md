## Special economic zones and small industrial zones

Special economic zones (SEZs) are governed by Law No. ZRU-604 "On Special Economic Zones" of 17 February 2020. A zone is created by the President for up to 30 years and may be extended. As of 1 April 2026 the country had 33 SEZs, 396 small industrial zones, 27 technoparks and 143 youth industrial and entrepreneurial zones.

### Becoming a participant

The investor applies to the zone directorate; the project must fit the zone's specialisation, and in free economic zones it must also produce goods not made in Uzbekistan or in short supply, unless it is fully export-oriented. Applications are reviewed within 10 working days. Status is acquired on signing an investment agreement with the directorate and entry in the register of participants; the registration fee is 45 BCV (~USD 1,650). Land is leased through an electronic auction, for up to 49 years, and the lease can be terminated if work does not start within six months.

### Tax and customs benefits

Participants are exempt from property tax and land tax for a period that depends on the investment: three years from USD 300,000, five years from USD 3 million, seven years from USD 5 million and 10 years from USD 10 million. The profit tax exemption runs for three years for USD 3 to 5 million, five years for USD 5 to 15 million and 10 years for USD 15 million or more (Tax Code, Art. 473). The profit tax term starts when the production facility is commissioned (Art. 474).

For companies that obtain participant status on or after 1 April 2026, the profit tax benefit takes the form of accelerated depreciation of fixed assets instead of an exemption, for the same periods. Existing participants keep their exemptions until they expire. Benefits apply only to the activities listed in the investment agreement.

Participants pay no customs duties, other than VAT and clearance fees, on construction materials not produced in Uzbekistan and on technological equipment without a local equivalent. Raw materials for export production are also exempt. Import VAT can be deferred for up to 120 days without interest or security. If tax law changes, participants may keep applying the rules in force at their registration for the term of their benefits, up to 10 years, except for excise goods. At least 90% of a participant's staff must be Uzbek citizens.

Small industrial zones lease plots and premises to smaller manufacturers; their extended tax holiday for exporters was abolished from January 2025.

:::box Recent changes
- Zone administrative boards were abolished; directorates now run the zones.
- Raw materials qualify for customs relief only under the "processing on customs territory" procedure.
:::

## IT Park residents

The IT Park (Technological Park of Software Products and Information Technologies) admits Uzbek legal entities carrying on listed IT activities. Until 1 January 2028 residents are exempt from all taxes, mandatory fund contributions and social tax, and their employees pay personal income tax at a flat 7.5% (Presidential Resolution No. PP-589). Residents also pay no customs duties, other than clearance fees, on equipment and software for their own needs that is not produced in Uzbekistan.

From 1 January 2028 to 1 January 2040 the Tax Code continues the exemption from all taxes except VAT, but only for residents whose export share exceeds 50% of total income, and for IT education providers that place half their graduates with exporting residents (Art. 483).

Presidential Resolution No. PP-388 of 26 December 2025 tightened the regime from 1 April 2026. Payment organisations, payment system operators, marketplaces and microfinance organisations lost access to the benefits. The minimum export share is 20% of total income from 1 April 2026 and 35% from 1 January 2027. Residents that meet it pay the park a monthly contribution of 1% of revenue; those that do not pay 2% (revenue of UZS 5 to 100 billion) or 3% (above UZS 100 billion).

### International Digital Technologies Centre

Constitutional Law No. ZRU-1169 of 19 August 2026 creates a separate International Digital Technologies Centre with its own legal regime until 2100; it is due to enter into force in early 2027; check the exact date on lex.uz. Participants' income from priority activities will be exempt from profit tax, and sales inside the centre and abroad will be zero-rated for VAT. Highly qualified foreign employees will pay no personal income tax. The tax chapter does not apply to IT Park residents.

## Creative Industry Park

The Creative Industry Park was established by Presidential Decree No. UP-61 of 26 March 2025 for businesses defined by Law No. ZRU-970 "On Creative Industry" of 3 October 2024: literature, crafts and applied arts, architecture and design, fine arts and related activities. From 1 May 2025 to 1 January 2031 residents pay personal income tax on employees' salaries and social tax at half the standard rates (Tax Code, Art. 483). With standard rates of 12% for both, this means 6%. Residents are turnover tax payers regardless of income and can also apply for interest-free loans and grants.

## Tashkent International Financial Centre

Constitutional Law No. ZRU-1158 of 13 July 2026 established the Tashkent International Financial Centre (TIFC), in force since 25 July 2026. It is not an SEZ. It is a territory with its own regulator (the Financial Services Authority), its own court (the Tashkent International Commercial Court, sitting in English) and its own law. TIFC decisions prevail over conflicting national law, and gaps are filled by English common law and equity. An SEZ changes the price of doing business; TIFC changes the law that governs it.

> An SEZ changes the price of doing business; TIFC changes the law that governs it.

### Who may participate

The centre is open to banking, asset management, Islamic finance, insurance, market infrastructure and digital-asset businesses, and to holding companies, special-purpose vehicles and professional firms. Holders of Uzbek financial licences can continue through a transitional subsidiary or branch registered within 60 days of activation. Firms licensed abroad can obtain a transitional licence where the TIFC has a cooperation arrangement with their regulator.

### Incentives as known

TIFC bodies and qualified participants are exempt from profit tax and social tax on income from financial services until 1 January 2076, subject to substance, governance and anti-money-laundering conditions. Members of groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more get no exemption and pay a qualified domestic minimum top-up tax at the standard 15% rate. Foreign employees whose main workplace is the centre need no work permit, and national currency control rules do not apply to participants' operations within the centre.

The centre is not yet operational. Its bodies have 12 months from entry into force, extendable by six, to adopt the founding resolutions, including the detailed tax, currency and residency rules. A manager of the centre was appointed by presidential decree in September 2026.

## High-technology products: 0% profit tax

Profit from selling products made with high technologies is taxed at 0% (Tax Code, Art. 337, item 10²), a rate added by Law No. ZRU-1104 with effect from 25 December 2025. The rate applies for three years from the date the high-tech production facility is commissioned, and only to income from those products. The qualifying productions are listed by presidential resolution. For the electrical engineering industry, the list is Annex 5 to Presidential Resolution No. PP-20 of 22 January 2025: 13 categories, including mobile phones and computers, semiconductors and chips, medical imaging equipment, robotics and drones, and batteries and inverters.

## Investment agreements and infrastructure incentives

Outside the zones, the main tool is an investment agreement with the Government under the Law on Investments and Investment Activities (No. ZRU-598 of 25 December 2019, Arts. 40 to 45). It is mandatory whenever the Government grants a foreign investor additional guarantees or support, which are reserved for priority sectors and export projects; tax benefits under it must be time-limited. Since 2026, individual tax incentives may be granted only by presidential decision, as a rate reduction of no more than 50% for no more than three years.

The state can also provide an investment subsidy by building the external engineering and utility networks to the project site at its own expense (Art. 36). An investment tax credit allows tax payments to be deferred and repaid in stages (Art. 35). A Cabinet of Ministers regulation governs state reimbursement of the cost of external infrastructure. Investors in projects worth at least USD 10 million can be allocated land directly by the Cabinet of Ministers rather than at auction (Land Code, Art. 24).

Enterprises with direct private foreign investment can be exempt from land tax and property tax for a period set by the President (Tax Code, Art. 471, as amended by Law ZRU-1108 from 1 January 2026). Article 472 sets the conditions, including a foreign share of at least 33% (15% for a JSC). The exclusion of Tashkent city and region comes from the presidential decrees, not from the Code.
