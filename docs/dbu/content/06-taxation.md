## The tax system at a glance
:::table Main taxes and rates, November 2026
| Tax | Rate | Who pays | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate income tax | 15% | Resident companies and permanent establishments | 20%: banks, mobile operators, markets, shopping centres. 0%: listed activities. |
| Value added tax | 12% | Companies above the turnover threshold, or voluntarily | 0% on exports. Simplified 6% option for retail, catering and services. |
| Turnover tax | 4% | Small businesses below the threshold | Replaces profit tax and VAT. 1% to 4% for retail. |
| Withholding tax | 10% / 20% | Uzbek payer, on income of non-residents | 10% dividends and interest, 6% freight, 20% other income. |
| Dividends to residents | 5% | Withheld by the paying company | Resident companies and individuals. |
| Personal income tax | 12% | Employees, withheld by the employer | Residents on worldwide income, non-residents on Uzbek income. |
| Social tax | 12% | Employer, on top of gross payroll | Also due on income of foreign seconded staff. |
| Property, land and water | varies | Owners and users | Set by type of asset, region and purpose of use. |
:::

Eleven taxes in the Tax Code, of which most companies deal with four: profit tax, VAT, and the two taxes on payroll.

Rates as of November 2026. Reduced treaty rates for dividends, interest and royalties are listed in Appendix 1.


Presidential decrees regularly add incentives for particular sectors and territories, such as special economic zones and the IT Park. An investor should check whether its activity or location changes the standard rate before relying on it.

## Corporate income tax

### Who pays

Resident companies pay corporate income tax on worldwide income. Permanent establishments of foreign companies pay it on income connected with their activity in Uzbekistan. Individual entrepreneurs above the turnover threshold, or those who opt in, pay it as well.

### Taxable income

The base is total income less deductible expenses. An expense is deductible when it is economically justified and supported by documents, and the standard of documentation is high. In practice most disputes with the tax authority are about the paper behind a deduction, not about the rate.

> Most disputes with the tax authority are about the paper behind a deduction, not about the rate.

### Rates

The general rate is 15% and applies to permanent establishments too. Banks, mobile operators and the owners of markets and shopping centres pay 20%. Several activities pay 0%: farming of own produce, the social sector, small renewable generation and, since 2026, the sale of products made with high technologies (Tax Code, Art. 337).

The high-tech rate applies for three years from commissioning, to production on a list approved by the President. E-commerce companies have paid the general 15% instead of 10% since 1 January 2026. A company whose income first exceeds UZS 10 billion may halve its rate for that year and the next while income stays below UZS 100 billion. A company leaving turnover tax for the first time from 2026 is exempt for one year, except on dividends and interest.

### Tax period

The tax year is the calendar year and the reporting period is the quarter. Profit is calculated on a cumulative basis from the start of the year, so a loss in one quarter reduces the tax due for the next.

Quarterly returns and payments are due by the 20th of the month after the quarter, and the annual return by 1 March. Companies with prior-year income above UZS 20 billion also pay monthly advances by the 23rd (Tax Code, Art. 340).

### Depreciation

Fixed assets are depreciated on a straight-line basis, at rates no higher than these:

:::table Maximum annual depreciation rates (Tax Code, Art. 306)
| Asset group | Rate |
|---|---|
| Buildings | 5% |
| Structures, aircraft, ships, rolling stock | 10% |
| Pipelines, power and telecom lines | 15% |
| Machinery and equipment, cars and trucks | 20% |
| Computers and peripherals | 40% |
| Other assets | 15% |
:::

Land and construction in progress are not depreciated. Intangible assets are amortised over their useful life, or over five years when it cannot be determined. A company may choose lower rates.

### Losses

A loss can be carried forward to later years without a time limit and without a cap on the share of profit it offsets. Losses from several years are used in the order they arose, and only against the result of a full tax year.

A loss on the sale of a shareholding or of a business as a whole can be set only against gains on similar assets (Tax Code, Art. 336).

### Thin capitalisation

Interest on debt owed to a foreign shareholder with more than 20%, or to its related parties, is limited once that debt exceeds three times the company's equity. For banks and leasing companies the threshold is thirteen times. The limit is recalculated at the end of every reporting period.

The rule also catches third-party loans guaranteed by the shareholder or its related parties (Tax Code, Art. 310).

### Investment deduction

A company may deduct 20% of the cost of new technological equipment, modernisation and domestically produced software, and 10% of spending on new production buildings and their reconstruction, in the period the asset is put into use.

Equipment is new if it was made no more than three years earlier, and the deduction is reversed if the asset is disposed of within three years (Tax Code, Art. 308).

### Controlled foreign companies

An Uzbek resident that controls a foreign company includes the company's retained profit in its own taxable income. Control starts at a holding of more than 25%, or more than 10% when Uzbek residents together hold more than half.

The profit is exempt for active companies outside the official offshore list, and for treaty-country companies that are licensed banks or insurers or pay an effective rate of at least 15%. Profit of UZS 300 million or less is ignored. Controlled companies are reported by 20 March of the following year (Tax Code, Arts. 204, 208, 209).

### Permanent establishments

Net profit left at the disposal of a permanent establishment after tax is treated like a dividend and taxed at 10%, unless a treaty sets a lower rate. Whether a foreign company has a permanent establishment at all is the first question to settle before any project starts.

Under domestic law a fixed place of business creates one, as does a building site or the provision of services through staff that lasts more than 183 days in any twelve months, with periods of related companies added together (Tax Code, Art. 36).

> Whether a foreign company has a permanent establishment at all is the first question to settle before any project starts.

:::box New in 2026
- 0% rate on profit from high-tech production.
- E-commerce companies move from 10% to 15%.
- Lower penalties for late and incorrect returns.
- Automated transfer-pricing control of related-party deals.
:::

## Withholding tax and treaties

### Withholding tax

Income that a foreign company without a permanent establishment earns from Uzbek sources is taxed at the source. Any Uzbek company that pays it acts as tax agent: it withholds the tax from the gross amount and pays it to the budget.

The rates are 10% on dividends, interest and insurance premiums, 6% on international freight and telecoms, and 20% on all other income, including fees for services. Interest on loans from foreign financial institutions that fund investment projects is taxed at 0% (Tax Code, Art. 353).

Management, technical and consulting fees are Uzbek-source income even when the work is done abroad (Tax Code, Art. 351). The tax is paid by the day after the payment and reported by the 20th of the following month. Since 2026, tax on dividends to a foreign shareholder holding at least 25% for 365 days can be reduced by tax already paid on dividends the payer itself received from Uzbek companies.

### Treaties

Uzbekistan has double tax treaties with more than fifty countries. Most reduce withholding on dividends, interest and royalties, and most exempt business profits that are not earned through a permanent establishment. A treaty rate applies only when the foreign recipient gives the payer a certificate of tax residence before the payment.

The certificate must be apostilled or legalised, or printed from the foreign tax authority's website (Tax Code, Art. 358). For dividends, interest and royalties the recipient must also be the beneficial owner. Tax withheld for lack of a certificate can later be reclaimed. A payer cannot apply a treaty exemption to service fees once the services create a permanent establishment, so long projects need the 183-day test monitored throughout.

:::example A consulting fee paid abroad
An Uzbek company pays a German consultant UZS 500 million for advice delivered in Tashkent. The consultant has no permanent establishment in Uzbekistan. VAT is paid by the Uzbek company as tax agent, on top of the fee.
| Item | Without a residence certificate | With a German residence certificate |
|---|---|---|
| Fee | 500 000 000 | 500 000 000 |
| Withholding tax | − 100 000 000 (20%) | 0 (treaty) |
| VAT as tax agent, 12% | 60 000 000 | 60 000 000 |
| Consultant receives | 400 000 000 | 500 000 000 |
:::

## Value added tax

### Registration and threshold

VAT is charged at 12% on supplies of goods and services in Uzbekistan and on imports. Payers deduct the VAT on their purchases from the VAT on their sales.

Every Uzbek company is a VAT payer unless it uses turnover tax, which since 1 June 2026 is possible up to annual income of 12,000 BCV (Presidential Decree UP-100). Above that level VAT applies from the day the threshold is crossed. Registration is made in the taxpayer's online account, and voluntary registration is possible. Returns are monthly, filed and paid by the 20th (Tax Code, Arts. 259, 273).

### Rates

The standard rate is 12%, with 0% for exports and other listed supplies (Tax Code, Art. 258). Since 1 June 2026 retail, catering and service businesses may instead pay 6% of turnover without input VAT credits, as described below.

### Zero rate and exemptions

Exports and international transport are zero-rated. Education, healthcare, financial services and a long list of social goods are exempt, and input VAT on exempt activity cannot be recovered.

A zero-rated exporter keeps its right to recover input VAT but must prove the export with customs and payment documents. Since 2026 sales by listed agricultural producers, except cotton and grain, are also zero-rated.

### Input VAT and invoices

Input VAT is deducted only on an electronic invoice from the state e-invoicing system. Since 1 January 2026 an automated system rates every invoice by risk, and VAT on a high-risk invoice is credited only once the supplier, or the buyer as tax agent, has paid it.

### Services from abroad

When a foreign company supplies services to an Uzbek business, the Uzbek business pays the VAT as tax agent and may then deduct it. If the contract price excludes VAT, the buyer adds 12% and pays it from its own funds, whether or not it is itself a VAT payer (Tax Code, Art. 255).

As a rule a service is supplied where the buyer is established, so most services bought by an Uzbek company are taxed in Uzbekistan. Services tied to real estate or performed on site follow their own rules (Tax Code, Art. 241).

### Foreign digital and e-commerce sellers

Foreign providers of digital services to consumers register themselves and pay VAT directly through the tax portal. They register within 30 days of their first sale and file and pay quarterly, by the 20th of the following month (Tax Code, Arts. 279–281). When the customer is an Uzbek company, the company pays as tax agent.

From 12 December 2026 the regime extends to goods. A foreign company selling goods through an electronic platform to individuals resident in Uzbekistan, with delivery there, becomes a VAT payer. If the sale runs through an Uzbek platform that settles with the seller, the platform pays the VAT as tax agent. A seller selling directly registers within 30 calendar days of its first sale and reports electronically through its online taxpayer account (Law ZRU-1173 of 10 September 2026).

:::box Foreign platforms from 12 December 2026
- Covers sales of goods to Uzbek consumers, delivered to Uzbekistan.
- Uzbek platforms that pay foreign sellers act as tax agents.
- Direct sellers register within 30 days of the first sale.
- Tax notices sent to foreign sellers count as received after 30 days.
:::

### Refunds

Excess input VAT is carried forward or refunded, and since 2026 small businesses receive refunds automatically: since 1 June, a balance of up to UZS 10 million is repaid without an application or audit.

Larger refunds are paid within 30 days of a desk audit (Tax Code, Art. 274). Large taxpayers, established exporters and those giving a bank guarantee are refunded within seven days, and businesses rated AAA within one day.

## Turnover tax and the small-business reform of 1 June 2026

### Thresholds

Turnover tax replaces profit tax and VAT for small businesses. Since 1 June 2026 a company may use it while annual income stays below 12,000 BCV, instead of UZS 1 billion (Presidential Decree UP-100 of 26 May 2026).

A company that crossed the old limit before 1 June 2026 stays on VAT for the rest of 2026, because a move to turnover tax takes effect only from the next tax year. It may switch from 2027 if its 2026 income stays below the new limit, giving notice at least ten days before the year starts (Tax Code, Art. 462). Turnover tax payers still pay social tax and property, land and water taxes.

### Turnover tax rates

:::table Turnover tax rates (Tax Code, Art. 467)
| Taxpayer | Rate |
|---|---|
| All sectors, unless listed below | 4% |
| Retail in cities of 100,000 people or more | 4% |
| Retail in other settlements | 2% |
| Retail in remote and mountain districts | 1% |
| Retail sales of tobacco, anywhere | 4% |
| Individual entrepreneurs and the self-employed | 1% |
| Procurement organisations for farm produce | 4% of turnover or 25% of gross income |
:::

Since 1 January 2026 individual entrepreneurs and the self-employed pay a single 1% on their turnover, and the fixed-amount income tax for entrepreneurs has been abolished (Tax Code, Arts. 383, 467).

Returns are monthly, filed and paid by the 15th, with an annual return by 15 February (Tax Code, Art. 470).

### Who cannot use turnover tax

The regime is closed to producers of excisable goods, mining companies, fuel sellers, markets and shopping centres, alcohol retailers, pharmacies and medical providers, jewellers, tax consultants, auditors and non-profit organisations, among others (Tax Code, Art. 461). Decree UP-100 ended the rule that importers must register for VAT. Whether importers may now use turnover tax is not settled, and should be confirmed with the Tax Committee before relying on it.

### The simplified 6% VAT option

From 1 June 2026 to 1 January 2030, a business whose main activity is retail, catering or services may pay VAT at 6% of total turnover instead of 12% on value added. Its profit tax rate is then 0%, but it cannot deduct input VAT and loses VAT refunds. Its customers can still deduct the 6% charged to them.

Exports are also taxed at 6%, while imports remain at 12%. The main activity is tested monthly by share of revenue. The switch takes effect from the month after notice is filed online.

### Who cannot use it

Large taxpayers and enterprises at least 50% owned by the state are excluded, and a business whose main activity is manufacturing does not qualify (Decree UP-100, para. 5). A business may leave from the first day of the month after notice, and then credits input VAT on its stock and fixed assets at book value.

:::box Choosing between 6% and 12%
- The 6% option suits high-margin businesses with few taxable inputs.
- Businesses with large VAT-bearing purchases usually pay less at 12%.
- Exporters lose the zero rate and refunds under 6%.
- Unused input VAT on hand is adjusted on entry.
:::

## Personal income tax and social tax

### Residency

An individual is tax resident if present in Uzbekistan for more than 183 days in any twelve-month period that begins or ends in the tax year (Tax Code, Art. 30). Residents are taxed on worldwide income, non-residents on Uzbek-source income only.

### Rates

Personal income tax is a flat 12% for residents. Non-residents pay 12% on employment and most other income and 10% on dividends and interest (Tax Code, Arts. 381, 382). The employer withholds the tax and files monthly by the 15th, with an annual report by 15 February.

### Dividends and interest

Residents pay 5% on dividends and interest, withheld by the payer. Interest on bank deposits is exempt. Individuals with income not taxed at source file a declaration by 1 April and pay by 1 June.

### Pension contribution

Employers transfer 0.1% of each employee's salary to an individual pension account. It is not an extra cost, because it is deducted from the income tax withheld (Tax Code, Art. 385). Published commentary says foreign citizens are covered only when permanently resident in Uzbekistan, but it dates from 2019 and 2020, so the position of each foreign employee should be confirmed.

### Social tax

Employers pay social tax of 12% on top of gross payroll (Tax Code, Art. 405), filed and paid monthly by the 15th (Art. 407). Temporary rates of 1% apply in some sectors, such as textiles.

### Foreign employees

Foreign employees of an Uzbek company are taxed like local staff. When a foreign company seconds staff under a service contract, the Uzbek recipient pays social tax on their pay, and at least on 90% of the contract value (Tax Code, Art. 404). Presidential Decree UP-180 of 4 October 2025 introduced a special regime that exempts only the foreign-source income of qualifying foreign citizens. It applies once the Tax Code is amended, when its procedure, Cabinet Resolution No. 270 of 21 May 2026, also takes effect. It does not change the tax on salaries paid by Uzbek employers.

:::example The full cost of an employee
An Uzbek company employs a resident specialist at a gross salary of UZS 10,000,000 a month. The employer withholds income tax, of which 0.1% of salary goes to the pension account, and pays social tax on top.
| Item | Employee | Employer |
|---|---|---|
| Gross salary | 10 000 000 | 10 000 000 |
| Personal income tax, 12% | − 1 200 000 | |
| incl. pension, 0.1% | 10 000 | |
| Social tax, 12% | | + 1 200 000 |
| Net salary / Total cost | 8 800 000 | 11 200 000 |
:::

## Property, land, water and subsoil taxes

Companies pay property tax at 1.5% of the average net book value of their real estate, but not less than a minimum value per square metre, UZS 3.53 million in Tashkent (Tax Code, Arts. 412, 415). Construction left unfinished beyond its standard term pays 3%, and railways, trunk pipelines and power lines 0.7%. The annual return is due by 1 March, with monthly advances by the 10th.

Land tax is a fixed amount per hectare set by region and adjusted locally, from UZS 65.9 million to UZS 319 million a year in Tashkent, after a 7% increase from 1 January 2026 (Tax Code, Art. 429). Rent for state land counts as land tax.

Water use tax for most businesses is UZS 749 per cubic metre from surface sources and UZS 910 from underground sources (Tax Code, Art. 445). Use above the permitted limit is taxed at five times the rate. Subsoil use tax is a percentage of the value extracted, for example 10% for oil and gas, 7% for gold and copper and 4% for coal (Tax Code, Art. 452). Producers of certain metals and hydrocarbons also pay a special rental tax, and the President may set higher profit tax rates for individual mining companies.

## Excise

Excise tax is due on the production and import of tobacco, alcohol, fuel, natural gas, polyethylene granules, sugary and energy drinks and crisps. Rates are fixed per unit or a share of value, and the return is due monthly by the 10th (Tax Code, Arts. 2891–2893, 292).

:::table Examples of excise rates, 2026
| Product | Rate |
|---|---|
| Cigarettes, from 1 July 2026 | UZS 365,000 per 1,000 |
| Vodka and cognac, from 1 July 2026 | UZS 48,000 per litre of pure alcohol |
| Petrol AI-91 and above, from 1 April 2026 | UZS 360,000 per tonne |
| Natural gas | 12% |
:::

## Tax administration

### Registration

A company is registered for tax automatically on incorporation. A foreign company with a permanent establishment registers within 30 days if its contract exceeds 183 days, and otherwise within 183 days (Tax Code, Art. 130). Operating without registration costs 10% of the income, at least UZS 10 million.

### E-invoicing

Invoices are issued only through the state e-invoicing system, and retail sales through online tills linked to the tax authority. Since 2026 the authority pre-fills VAT, turnover tax, payroll, property tax and land tax returns (Tax Code, Art. 81). The taxpayer can correct them within five days, but no later than the filing deadline, and is otherwise deemed to agree.

### Filing and payment calendar

Most returns are monthly, due on the 10th, 15th or 20th, and profit tax is quarterly. The Tax Calendar appendix lists every deadline.

### Audits

Desk audits review returns. On-site inspections last up to ten days. Full tax audits target high-risk taxpayers, are announced 30 days ahead and last 30 days, extendable to six months (Tax Code, Arts. 137–142). Tax can be reassessed for three years after the end of the period (Tax Code, Art. 88).

Businesses rated AAA for sustainability are not audited, except in criminal cases. Until 1 January 2028 a small business is not audited when the tax at risk is below UZS 500 million, nor inspected on site below UZS 100 million, and may correct errors found by automated review itself.

### Penalties and the 2026 changes

Underpaid tax attracts a penalty of 20%, a concealed tax base 20% of the base and non-market transfer pricing 40% of the tax (Tax Code, Arts. 223–226). Penalties are halved for prompt payment with admission. Late payment interest is 1/300 of the Central Bank refinancing rate per day.

From 2026 the fine for late returns by small business officials fell from 10 to 3 base units, and a return filed within five working days is not fined after three punctual months. At the same time Law ZRU-1131 of 17 April 2026, in force since 19 July 2026, raised fines and criminal penalties for concealing income.

:::box Recent changes to penalties
- Late-return fine for small business officials: 3 base units, not 10.
- One fine for all returns late in the same month.
- No fine for late VAT registration in the first year after leaving turnover tax.
- Higher fines for concealment and tax evasion.
:::

### Appeals

Audit decisions can be appealed to the higher tax authority within one month, and the appeal suspends collection (Tax Code, Arts. 231, 232). The authority decides within one month, extendable by 15 days. The taxpayer may also go directly to court, and decisions of the Tax Committee itself can be challenged only in court. If a decision is cancelled, tax and penalties collected are returned with interest at the refinancing rate.

## Transfer pricing

### Controlled transactions

The rules cover transactions between related parties, foreign trade in exchange-traded commodities such as metals, fertilisers, oil products and cotton, and any deal with a party in a listed offshore jurisdiction (Tax Code, Arts. 180, 181).

### Thresholds

Cross-border related-party transactions are controlled whatever their size. Deals between related Uzbek residents are controlled above UZS 5 billion a year, or UZS 500 million when one side enjoys a special regime or reduced rate.

### Documentation

Controlled transactions are notified by the deadline for annual financial statements (Tax Code, Art. 182). Documentation is not filed in advance, but can be requested from 1 June of the following year and must then be provided within 30 days (Tax Code, Arts. 193, 195). It should describe the transaction, the functions, assets and risks of each party, the pricing method and the comparables used. A transfer-pricing audit lasts up to six months, extendable to twelve, and advance pricing agreements are available.

### Automated control

In May 2026 the Tax Committee announced that it is introducing an automated system that identifies related parties, lists controlled transactions, tests prices against the market and flags risks. Companies should expect questions on related-party prices before any audit starts.

## Global minimum tax (Pillar Two)

Uzbekistan joined the OECD Inclusive Framework in 2023, but has not introduced the 15% global minimum tax.

:::box Does Pillar Two affect Uzbek subsidiaries?
- The Tax Code has no income inclusion rule or domestic minimum tax.
- The rules apply to groups with revenue of EUR 750 million or more.
- At 15%, the standard rate meets the minimum.
- Subsidiaries on 0% rates or incentives may trigger top-up tax abroad.
:::
