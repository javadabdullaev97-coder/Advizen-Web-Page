## Legal framework

Accounting is governed by the Law on Accounting (No. ZRU-404 of 13 April 2016) and by national accounting standards (NSBU) issued by the Ministry of Economy and Finance. The Central Bank sets the rules for banks. Every legal entity registered in Uzbekistan must keep accounts, including its branches, representative offices and other subdivisions inside or outside the country.

The director is responsible for organising accounting. The director may set up an in-house function under a chief accountant, hire an accountant under a services contract, outsource to an audit firm, tax consultancy or accounting firm, or keep the books personally (Law on Accounting, Art. 11). In audited companies the chief accountant must hold a degree and meet experience requirements.

New editions of NSBU 1 (accounting policy and presentation), NSBU 22 (foreign currency) and a new chart of accounts (NSBU 21, Order No. 191 of 14 November 2024) took effect on 1 January 2025. Small businesses may use simplified accounting under NSBU 20.

## From national standards to IFRS

Presidential Resolution PP-282 of 15 September 2025, in force since 17 December 2025, splits reporting into three tiers. Full IFRS applies to public-interest entities. Other businesses move to national financial reporting standards (NSFO) built on IFRS for SMEs. Any entity may adopt full IFRS voluntarily.

PP-282 repealed the paragraph of PP-4611 of 24 February 2020 that required joint-stock companies, commercial banks, insurers and large taxpayers to apply IFRS, so those outside the register no longer have to. NSFO will be introduced in phases from 1 January 2027 (PP-282, roadmap item 8). NSFO No. 2 (preparation basis, accounting policies and estimates; reg. No. 3810 of 11 April 2026) and NSFO No. 1 (presentation and disclosure; reg. No. 3923 of 13 August 2026) both apply from 1 January 2027. They cover all legal entities except public-interest entities and budget organisations.

### The public-interest register

The Ministry of Economy and Finance keeps the register and publishes it on the government portal. An entity qualifies if it is listed on a stock exchange, is a commercial bank, microfinance institution or payment service provider, is an investment fund or an exchange, or is a large enterprise with state participation. A private company also qualifies if, in each of the two preceding years, it had assets and net revenue of at least 1,000,000 BCV (~USD 37 million) each and at least 500 employees.

The first register, published in June 2026, listed 425 organisations. An update of 12 October 2026 raised the total to 449. An entity added in 2026 keeps its books under IFRS from 1 January 2027 and publishes audited IFRS statements for 2028. Entities whose IFRS deadline was already earlier keep that date. IFRS sustainability disclosure standards also become mandatory for public-interest entities from 1 January 2027.

:::table Who uses which standards
| Entity | Framework | Effective |
|---|---|---|
| Public-interest entities | Full IFRS | Year after inclusion in register |
| Banks and credit organisations | Central Bank rules and IFRS | Already in force |
| Medium and other companies | NSBU, then NSFO | NSFO phased from 1 January 2027 |
| Small enterprises | Simplified NSBU 20, then NSFO | NSFO phased from 1 January 2027 |
| Any entity | Full IFRS, voluntary | Notify tax authority by 1 March |
:::

## Statutory audit

Under the Law on Auditing Activity (No. ZRU-677 of 25 February 2021, Art. 35), an annual audit is mandatory for joint-stock companies, banks and credit organisations, insurers, agricultural cooperatives, investment funds and their managers, exchanges, charitable and public funds, and companies with a state share. Other commercial companies must be audited if, in the reporting year, they meet two of three criteria:

- Book value of assets above 100,000 BCV (~USD 3.7 million).
- Sales revenue above 200,000 BCV (~USD 7.3 million).
- Average headcount above 100.

A copy of the audit report goes to the tax authority within 15 days of completion and no later than 15 June of the following year. Joint-stock companies, banks and insurers must publish audited annual statements at least two weeks before the annual general meeting (Law on Accounting, Art. 26). At the end of 2025 there were 175 audit organisations in the register.

> An entity added in 2026 keeps its books under IFRS from 1 January 2027 and publishes audited IFRS statements for 2028.

## Financial statements

The reporting year is the calendar year. A company registered after 1 October closes its first reporting year on 31 December of the following year. A full set comprises a balance sheet, income statement, cash flow statement, statement of changes in equity and notes. Micro firms and small enterprises file only the balance sheet and income statement.

Annual statements under NSBU are due by 1 March and IFRS statements by 1 May of the following year (Ministry Order No. 181 of 8 October 2024). They go electronically to the tax and statistics authorities. Quarterly statements were abolished from 1 January 2023. Turnover tax payers, exempt for 2023 to 2025, file again from the 2026 reporting year. Late filing exposes officials to fines of 3 to 7 BCV (~USD 110 to 260).

## Accounting policy

The director approves the accounting policy by order. Under NSBU 1 it may not be changed during the calendar year except in the cases the standard lists. The Tax Code also requires a tax accounting policy, in free form, covering tax registers, depreciation rates and expense methods (Tax Code, Art. 77). The two are often combined in one document.

:::box Practical tip
- Approve both policies before the first transaction; changes normally apply from 1 January.
- Check each year whether the company has crossed the audit or public-interest thresholds.
:::

## Records, language and currency

Accounts are kept in the national currency, the sum (Law on Accounting, Art. 17). Foreign currency transactions are recorded at the Central Bank rate on the transaction date, and monetary items are revalued at month end (NSBU 22). Under the Law on the State Language (Art. 10), accounting and financial records are kept in Uzbek, and other languages may be used alongside it where most staff do not speak Uzbek. Accounting documents must be kept for at least five years after the reporting year (Law on Accounting, Art. 29).

## Electronic documents

Primary documents and accounting registers may be electronic (Law on Accounting, Arts. 14 and 15). Invoices are normally issued in the state electronic invoicing system (Tax Code, Art. 47). Reports filed electronically since 1 January 2022 no longer need to be archived on paper (PP-419 of 10 November 2022).
