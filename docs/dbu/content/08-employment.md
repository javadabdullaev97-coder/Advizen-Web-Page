## Legal framework

Employment is governed by the Labour Code (Law No. ZRU-798 of 28 October 2022), in force since 30 April 2023, and by the Law on Employment. The Code applies to everyone working under an employment contract in Uzbekistan, whatever their citizenship and whatever the ownership of the employer. Collective agreements, local acts and individual contracts may improve on the statutory minimum but may not reduce it.

The Code is amended often. Changes since 2025 include electronic contracts (Law No. ZRU-1030), a new overtime pay scale (ZRU-1095), revised severance rules (ZRU-1070) and, from 12 September 2026, strike rules and part-time work on request for parents and carers (ZRU-1150). The State Labour Inspectorate of the Ministry of Employment and Poverty Reduction supervises compliance and since 1 September 2025 inspects without notice (Decree UP-126).

## Employment contracts

### Form and registration

An employment contract must be in writing, on paper or electronically, in at least two copies signed by both parties (Labour Code, Art. 106). An electronic contract is concluded in the Unified National Labour System, the state platform that also holds each worker's electronic labour book. The conclusion, amendment and termination of every contract must be registered in the system with the employer's digital signature (Art. 109), a paper contract within five working days and a termination within three (Cabinet Resolution No. 971). The electronic labour book is the main record of an employee's service (Art. 125).

### Fixed-term and indefinite contracts

A contract is either indefinite or fixed-term for up to three years (Art. 110). A fixed-term contract is lawful only in the cases listed in Articles 112 and 113, for example temporary work or replacing an absent employee. Including extensions, it may not run longer than five years. A fixed-term contract concluded without proper grounds is treated as indefinite, and in a dispute the employer must prove that the fixed term was justified.

### Probation

A probation period may not exceed three months. For heads of organisations, their deputies, chief accountants and heads of separate subdivisions the limit is six months (Art. 130). Periods of sick leave and other absence do not count towards probation. During probation either party may end the contract with three days' written notice (Art. 132).

## Working time and leave

The normal working week may not exceed 40 hours, on a five-day or six-day schedule (Art. 182). Shorter hours are mandatory for some groups: employees aged 16 to 18 work no more than 36 hours a week.

Overtime requires the employee's written consent, except in emergencies listed in the Code. It may not exceed four hours over two consecutive days, or two hours a day in adverse working conditions (Art. 190). Overtime is not allowed on 12-hour shifts. Since a November 2025 amendment (Law No. ZRU-1095), the first two hours of overtime in a day are paid at least 150% of the normal rate and further hours at least 200% (Art. 262). The same law abolished the general annual cap of 120 overtime hours. Work on a rest day or public holiday is paid at least double.

The minimum annual leave is 21 calendar days (Art. 217). Employees earn two extra calendar days for every five years with the same employer or in the same industry, up to eight days in total (Art. 220). Employees under 18 and employees with group I or II disabilities receive 30 calendar days (Art. 218).

### Sick pay

Since 1 July 2026, temporary disability benefit is assigned automatically through the State Social Insurance Fund under Presidential Decree UP-206 of 3 November 2025. The employer pays for the first five days of incapacity in each calendar year, and the fund pays from the sixth day (Cabinet Resolution No. 796 of 17 December 2025, Annex 4, para. 20). The benefit is 60% of average earnings for an insurance record of six months to eight years and 80% above eight years, based on earnings up to ten times the minimum wage (Cabinet Resolution No. 239 of 12 May 2026). It is paid for up to 182 calendar days a year.

## Termination

A contract ends by agreement, on expiry of its term, at the initiative of either party, or on other grounds in the Code (Art. 155). An employee may resign with 14 calendar days' written notice (Art. 160). A chief executive must give two months' notice, and deputies and chief accountants one month (Art. 489). Since 1 November 2025 Decree UP-126 extends the micro-firm rules to employers with up to 50 staff: seven days' notice of resignation, notice and severance set by contract, and optional local acts.

Dismissal at the employer's initiative must be justified by one of the grounds in Article 161:

- Liquidation of the organisation or a separate subdivision.
- Reduction of headcount or staff for technological or organisational reasons.
- Insufficient qualification for the position.
- Repeated breach of duties within a year of an earlier disciplinary measure.
- A single gross breach of duties.

Other laws may add grounds, such as ZRU-1180 for education staff. Union consent is needed only under a collective agreement (Art. 164). A pregnant employee may be dismissed only on liquidation, and a mother of a child under three or a single father only on liquidation or for misconduct (Arts. 408, 409).

Before dismissing for redundancy or lack of qualification, the employer must offer any suitable vacancy (Art. 144). Dismissal for misconduct is a disciplinary sanction and must follow the disciplinary procedure.

### Notice and severance

The employer must give written notice of at least two months for liquidation or redundancy, two weeks for insufficient qualification, and three days for misconduct (Art. 165). Notice may be replaced by pay in lieu. During notice, except for misconduct, the employee may take at least one paid day a week to look for work.

Severance is due on dismissal at the employer's initiative, except for misconduct, and on several other no-fault grounds (Art. 173). The minimum depends on service with the employer.

:::table Minimum severance pay (Labour Code, Art. 173)
| Service with employer | Severance, share of average monthly pay |
|---|---|
| Up to 3 years | 50% |
| 3 to 5 years | 75% |
| 5 to 10 years | 100% |
| 10 to 15 years | 150% |
| Over 15 years | 200% |
:::

> Before dismissing for redundancy or lack of qualification, the employer must offer any suitable vacancy.

## Minimum wage and base calculation value

Presidential Decree UP-115 of 23 June 2026 raised both reference values from 1 September 2026:

- Minimum monthly wage: UZS 1,360,000, up from UZS 1,271,000.
- Base calculation value (BCV): UZS 440,000 (~USD 37), up from UZS 412,000.

The minimum wage is the floor for full-time pay. The BCV is the unit for fines, state fees and many thresholds in this guide.

The employer withholds and pays payroll taxes monthly, as set out in the Taxation chapter.

## Remote work

The Code treats remote work as ordinary employment with special rules (Arts. 452 to 464). It may be permanent, temporary or combined with office work. Temporary remote work requires the employee's consent and may last no more than one year (Art. 453). Without consent, an employee may be moved only in emergencies such as epidemics or disasters (Art. 454). The contract must state how equipment is provided and repaired, how communication and other costs are reimbursed and how tasks are exchanged (Art. 456).

## Foreign employees

### Work permits

A foreign national who enters Uzbekistan to work needs a confirmation of the right to work, the local form of work permit (Labour Code, Art. 523). The employer may sign the contract only once the permit is issued, and the contract may not run longer than the permit (Art. 524). It ends automatically when the permit expires or is annulled (Art. 525). Foreign workers must be at least 18.

The employer applies at a Public Services Centre or through my.gov.uz. The local labour office checks for local candidates within five working days, and the ministry responsible for employment, currently the Ministry of Employment and Poverty Reduction, decides within 15 working days (Law on Employment, Art. 111). A standard permit is valid for the contract term, up to one year, and can be renewed.

### Qualified and highly qualified specialists

Two categories receive better terms under the Law on Employment (Art. 109) and Presidential Resolution PP-4008 of 7 November 2018:

- Highly qualified specialist: degree from a university on the official top 1,000 list of the Ministry of Higher Education, five years' experience and pay of at least USD 60,000 a year.
- Qualified specialist: higher education, five years' experience and pay of at least USD 30,000 a year.

Their permits run for up to three years and can be extended without limit, for up to three years each time (Art. 526). They may also work part-time for another employer without a separate permit. If the contract ends early, they have 30 working days to find a new employer, while their permit, visa and residence permit stay valid.

### Exemptions and quotas

No work permit is needed by residence permit holders, foreign staff of accredited representative offices (at most 40% of staff and five people), founders of foreign and joint enterprises, including founders who head them, for up to three months, diplomats, journalists and some other groups (Law on Employment, Art. 110). Annex 20 to Cabinet Resolution No. 86 adds investors of at least 8,500 BCV (~USD 310,000), specialists of IT Park residents, Vatandosh card holders and teachers at Presidential Schools and universities. Since Cabinet Resolution No. 541 of 28 September 2022, people working for an Uzbek employer from abroad are also exempt while abroad.

There is no numerical quota on foreign workers. Instead, the authorities check that the number and positions requested match the employer's actual needs, and may refuse a permit only on the grounds listed in the law (Law on Employment, Art. 111).

:::box Practical tip
- Budget at least three weeks for a permit, then add time for the work visa (see Entry, Visas and Residence).
- Set the salary in the contract at or above the USD threshold for the category you claim.
- Diarise renewals well ahead: the contract ends on the day the permit expires.
:::

## Employer of Record

A foreign company without a local entity may in law employ staff directly (Labour Code, Art. 12), but registration, tax agent duties (Tax Code, Art. 386) and permanent establishment risk make it impractical. An Employer of Record (EoR) solves this through outstaffing (Law on Employment, Art. 16, and Cabinet Resolution No. 706 of 25 October 2024). A local company employs the worker and runs payroll, while the client directs the daily work. An assignment lasts up to nine months a calendar year, extendable by agreement, pay may not fall below that of the client's own staff, and the client is secondarily liable for wages.

The model suits small teams and market entry, because an Uzbek citizen can start within days, while foreign staff still need a work permit. For a larger long-term team, or one that will hold assets and sign contracts, a subsidiary or representative office is usually more economical.
