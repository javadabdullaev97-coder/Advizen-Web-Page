## Legal framework

Employment is governed by the Labour Code (Law No. ZRU-798 of 28 October 2022), in force since 30 April 2023, and by the Law on Employment. The Code applies to everyone working under an employment contract in Uzbekistan, whatever their citizenship and whatever the ownership of the employer. Collective agreements, local acts and individual contracts may improve on the statutory minimum but may not reduce it.

The Code is amended often. Changes since 2025 include electronic employment contracts, a new overtime pay scale and revised severance rules. The State Labour Inspectorate of the Ministry of Employment and Poverty Reduction supervises compliance.

## Employment contracts

### Form and registration

An employment contract must be in writing, on paper or electronically, in at least two copies signed by both parties (Labour Code, Art. 106). An electronic contract is concluded in the Unified National Labour System, the state platform that also holds each worker's electronic labour book. The conclusion, amendment and termination of every contract must be registered in the system (Art. 109). The electronic labour book is the main record of an employee's service (Art. 125).

### Fixed-term and indefinite contracts

A contract is either indefinite or fixed-term for up to three years (Art. 110). A fixed-term contract is lawful only in the cases listed in Articles 112 and 113, for example temporary work or replacing an absent employee. Including extensions, it may not run longer than five years. A fixed-term contract concluded without proper grounds is treated as indefinite, and in a dispute the employer must prove that the fixed term was justified.

### Probation

A probation period may not exceed three months. For heads of organisations, their deputies, chief accountants and heads of separate subdivisions the limit is six months (Art. 130). Periods of sick leave and other absence do not count towards probation. During probation either party may end the contract with three days' written notice (Art. 132).

## Working time and leave

The normal working week may not exceed 40 hours, on a five-day or six-day schedule (Art. 182). Shorter hours are mandatory for some groups: employees aged 16 to 18 work no more than 36 hours a week.

Overtime requires the employee's written consent, except in emergencies listed in the Code. It may not exceed four hours over two consecutive days, or two hours a day in adverse working conditions (Art. 190). Overtime is not allowed on 12-hour shifts. Since a November 2025 amendment (Law No. ZRU-1095), the first two hours of overtime are paid at least 150% of the normal rate and further hours at least 200% (Art. 262). The same law abolished the general annual cap of 120 overtime hours. Work on a rest day or public holiday is paid at least double.

The minimum annual leave is 21 calendar days (Art. 217). Employees earn two extra calendar days for every five years with the same employer or in the same industry, up to eight days in total (Art. 220). Employees under 18 and employees with group I or II disabilities receive 30 calendar days (Art. 218).

### Sick pay

Since 1 July 2026, temporary disability benefit is assigned automatically through the State Social Insurance Fund under Presidential Decree UP-206 of 3 November 2025. The employer pays for the first five days of incapacity in each calendar year, and the fund pays from the sixth day (Cabinet Resolution No. 796 of 17 December 2025, Annex 4, para. 20). The benefit is 60% of average earnings for an insurance record of six months to eight years and 80% above eight years, based on earnings up to ten times the minimum wage. It is paid for up to 182 calendar days a year.

## Termination

A contract ends by agreement, on expiry of its term, at the initiative of either party, or on other grounds in the Code (Art. 155). An employee may resign with 14 calendar days' written notice (Art. 160). A chief executive must give two months' notice, and deputies and chief accountants one month (Art. 489).

Dismissal at the employer's initiative must be justified by one of the grounds in Article 161:

- Liquidation of the organisation or a separate subdivision.
- Reduction of headcount or staff for technological or organisational reasons.
- Insufficient qualification for the position.
- Repeated breach of duties within a year of an earlier disciplinary measure.
- A single gross breach of duties.

Before dismissing for redundancy or lack of qualification, the employer must offer any suitable vacancy (Art. 144). Dismissal for misconduct is a disciplinary sanction and must follow the disciplinary procedure.

### Notice and severance

The employer must give written notice of at least two months for liquidation or redundancy, two weeks for insufficient qualification, and three days for misconduct (Art. 165). Notice may be replaced by pay in lieu. During notice, except for misconduct, the employee may take at least one paid day a week to look for work.

Severance is due on dismissal at the employer's initiative, except for misconduct, and on several other no-fault grounds (Art. 173). The minimum depends on service with the employer.

:::table Minimum severance pay (Labour Code, Art. 173)
| Service with employer | Severance, share of average monthly pay |
|---|---|
| Up to 3 years | 50% |
| 3 to 5 years | 75% |
| 5 to 10 years | 100% |
| 10 to 15 years | 150% |
| Over 15 years | 200% |
:::

> Before dismissing for redundancy or lack of qualification, the employer must offer any suitable vacancy.

## Minimum wage and base calculation value

Presidential Decree UP-115 of 23 June 2026 raised both reference values from 1 September 2026:

- Minimum monthly wage: UZS 1,360,000, up from UZS 1,271,000.
- Base calculation value (BCV): UZS 440,000 (~USD 37), up from UZS 412,000.

The minimum wage is the floor for full-time pay. The BCV is the unit for fines, state fees and many thresholds in this guide, including work permit fees.

Payroll taxes and social contributions are withheld and paid monthly by the employer; the rates are set out in the Taxation chapter.

## Remote work

The Code treats remote work as ordinary employment with special rules (Arts. 452 to 459). It may be permanent, temporary or combined with office work. Temporary remote work requires the employee's consent, except in emergencies such as epidemics or disasters (Art. 454), and may last no more than one year, after which the employee returns to the previous arrangement. Moving an employee to remote work under the same job is a change in working conditions and must be documented. The contract should state how equipment and software are provided and how costs are reimbursed.

## Foreign employees

### Work permits

A foreign national who enters Uzbekistan to work needs a confirmation of the right to work, the local form of work permit (Labour Code, Art. 523). The employer may sign the contract only once the permit is issued, and the contract may not run longer than the permit (Art. 524). It ends automatically when the permit expires or is annulled (Art. 525). Foreign workers must be at least 18.

The employer applies electronically with a digital signature, and the Ministry of Employment and Poverty Reduction reviews the application within 15 working days (Law on Employment, Art. 111). The application fee is 1 BCV (~USD 37). The issue fee is 1 BCV for highly qualified specialists, 2 BCV (~USD 75) for qualified specialists and 30 BCV (~USD 1,100) for other workers. A standard permit is valid for the contract term, up to one year, and can be renewed.

### Qualified and highly qualified specialists

Two categories receive better terms under Presidential Resolution PP-4008 of 7 November 2018:

- Highly qualified specialist: degree from a university in the top 1,000 of the QS, THE or ARWU rankings, five years' experience and pay of at least USD 60,000 a year.
- Qualified specialist: higher education, five years' experience and pay of at least USD 30,000 a year.

Their permits run for up to three years and can be extended without limit, for up to three years each time (Art. 526). They may also work part-time for another employer without a separate permit. If the contract ends early, they have 30 working days to find a new employer, while their permit, visa and residence permit stay valid.

### Exemptions and quotas

No work permit is needed by holders of an Uzbek residence permit, by staff of accredited representative offices of foreign commercial organisations, by founders of foreign and joint enterprises on visits of up to three months, or by several other groups such as accredited diplomats and journalists (Law on Employment, Art. 110). Since 1 July 2022, foreign nationals who work online for an Uzbek employer from abroad also need no permit.

There is no numerical quota on foreign workers. Instead, the authorities check that the number and positions requested match the employer's actual needs, and may refuse a permit only on the grounds listed in the law (Law on Employment, Art. 111).

:::box Practical tip
- Budget at least three weeks for a permit, then add time for the work visa.
- Set the salary in the contract at or above the USD threshold for the category you claim.
- Diarise renewals well ahead: the contract ends on the day the permit expires.
:::

### Visas and residence

Workers from visa-regime countries need a work visa. Qualified and highly qualified specialists can receive multiple-entry work visas for the contract term, up to three years, and can extend them without leaving the country. Highly qualified specialists and their family members may also obtain residence permits for the term of the contract. Since 1 June 2025, any foreign national can obtain a five-year residence permit under a simplified procedure for a fee of USD 250,000, plus USD 150,000 per family member (Decree UP-67 of 18 April 2025). The Entry, Visas and Residence chapter has the details.

## Employer of Record

A foreign company without a local entity cannot normally employ staff in Uzbekistan directly. An Employer of Record (EoR) solves this: a local company signs the employment contract, registers it in the Unified National Labour System, runs payroll, withholds tax and contributions, and handles leave and termination. The foreign client directs the employee's daily work.

The model suits small teams and market entry, because onboarding takes days rather than the weeks needed to register a company. For a larger long-term team, or where the business will hold assets and sign local contracts, a subsidiary or representative office is usually more economical. The service agreement should allocate employment risk clearly and be reviewed for permanent establishment exposure.
