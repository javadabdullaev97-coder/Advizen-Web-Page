## The Customs Code and customs regimes

Customs matters are governed by the Customs Code of 20 January 2016, last amended in July 2026. It is administered by the Customs Committee under the Ministry of Economy and Finance. Detailed procedures are set by Cabinet of Ministers resolutions.

Goods crossing the border must be placed under one of 16 customs regimes (Customs Code, Art. 25), which decide the payments due and what may be done with the goods:

- Export, re-export, temporary export and processing outside the customs territory.
- Release for free circulation (import), re-import, temporary import and processing within the customs territory.
- Temporary storage, customs warehouse, free warehouse and free customs zone.
- Duty-free trade, customs transit, destruction and abandonment to the state.

A declarant may choose any regime and later switch to another (Art. 26). Companies with a clean record can become authorised economic operators and use simplified procedures (Art. 7-1).

## Import payments

### Customs duty

Import duty rates are set by Presidential Resolution PP-3818 of 29 June 2018, as amended. Most are ad valorem on the customs value and reach up to 70%. Many staple foods, children's clothing and footwear, and textile inputs not made locally, are duty-free until 1 January 2027.

The standard rates apply to goods from countries that grant Uzbekistan most-favoured-nation (MFN) treatment. Since 28 February 2026, other goods, and goods of unproven origin, pay an additional duty instead of the former double rate (Customs Code, Art. 300-1):

:::table Additional duty on goods without MFN treatment or proof of origin
| Standard ad valorem rate | Additional duty |
|---|---|
| Up to 10% | 5% |
| 10% to 20% | 10% |
| 20% to 30% | 15% |
| 30% and above | 20% |
:::

### VAT and excise tax

Imports bear VAT at 12% (Tax Code, Art. 258) on the customs value plus duty and excise tax (Art. 254). Excisable goods such as tobacco, alcohol, fuel and sugary drinks also pay excise tax at the Art. 289 rates, many of them fixed per unit.

### Customs clearance fees

Clearance fees are set in multiples of the base calculation value (BCV, ~USD 37 since 1 September 2026). The scale in Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 55 of 31 January 2025, as amended from 8 February 2026, applies equally to exports and imports, in line with WTO rules.

:::table Clearance fee per goods declaration (Resolution No. 55)
| Customs value of goods | Fee |
|---|---|
| Up to USD 10,000 | 1 BCV (~USD 37) |
| USD 10,000 to 20,000 | 1.5 BCV (~USD 55) |
| USD 20,000 to 40,000 | 2.5 BCV (~USD 90) |
| USD 40,000 to 60,000 | 4 BCV (~USD 150) |
| USD 60,000 to 100,000 | 7 BCV (~USD 260) |
| USD 100,000 to 200,000 | 10 BCV (~USD 370) |
| USD 200,000 to 500,000 | 15 BCV (~USD 550) |
| USD 500,000 to 1 million | 20 BCV (~USD 740) |
| USD 1 million and above | 25 BCV (~USD 920) |
:::

Transit and processing declarations, and clearance outside working hours, cost 25% of the BCV (~USD 9) each. Declaring goods before arrival earns a 20% discount. The courier shipment fee fell from 2% to 1.6% of the BCV (~USD 0.60) per kilogram on 1 November 2026. Presidential Decree UP-174 of 27 August 2026 cuts fees for export clearance, phytosanitary certificates, fumigation and certificates of origin by 30% from 1 October 2026. By November 2026 the Resolution No. 55 scale had not yet been amended to match.

:::box Practical tip
- The fee is charged per declaration, so consolidating shipments lowers the cost per unit.
- With preliminary, periodic or incomplete declarations, the fee is paid once, on the first declaration.
- Fees rise automatically whenever the BCV is indexed.
:::

## Origin and trade agreements

Where several countries take part in making a product, its origin is where it was sufficiently processed (Customs Code, Art. 360): a change of tariff heading at the four-digit level, or a set share of added value. Simple assembly, repacking and sorting do not count. Preferences need a certificate of origin, which may be filed up to one year after declaration to reclaim the extra duty.

Uzbekistan joined the CIS Free Trade Zone Agreement in 2014, so goods originating in other member states are generally duty-free. The CIS agreement on free trade in services, establishment and investment entered into force for Uzbekistan on 21 June 2026. Uzbekistan has been an observer to the Eurasian Economic Union since 2020. It keeps its own tariff and does not apply the EAEU common external tariff.

Other preferential arrangements:

- Türkiye: a preferential trade agreement covering 12 products on each side. A protocol signed on 29 October 2026 adds eight more products on each side once both countries ratify it.
- European Union: GSP+ status since 2021, giving duty-free access for about two-thirds of EU tariff lines. Under the new EU GSP regulation, which applies from 1 January 2027, Uzbekistan must reapply by 31 December 2028 to keep GSP+ after 2028.

## Product labelling: Asl Belgisi

Many goods must carry a Data Matrix code from the state labelling system Asl Belgisi, operated by CRPT Turon. The categories include alcohol, tobacco, beer, household appliances, medicines and medical devices, bottled water and soft drinks, fertilisers and plant protection chemicals. Importers must apply codes before release into circulation, and electronic invoices must show them.

Since 1 March 2026, a permissive regime has applied at the till under Presidential Resolution PP-296 of 6 October 2025. Each code is scanned and checked against the Asl Belgisi database before a receipt is issued. The sale is blocked if the product has expired, the code is not scanned or the code has already been used. Since 1 July 2026, the code must appear on the online receipt.

## Certification and licensing

Listed products need a certificate or declaration of conformity before release for free circulation; goods for processing and exhibition samples are exempt. Foreign certificates are recognised only through an accredited Uzbek body or a mutual recognition agreement. Medicines and medical devices must be registered, and food, plant and animal products need sanitary, phytosanitary or veterinary clearance.

Presidential Decree UP-25 of 16 February 2026 moves conformity assessment to a risk-based approach. From 1 January 2027 it introduces a national conformity mark, Conformity Uzbekistan (CUz), for products covered by technical regulations (para. 6). A few goods, such as weapons and certain chemicals, still need an import or export licence.

## WTO accession

As of November 2026, Uzbekistan is not a WTO member. At the Working Party's 13th meeting on 27 and 28 July 2026, 31 bilateral market access agreements had been deposited, most recently with India. Members still raised questions on foreign exchange, import rules, technical barriers, state-owned enterprises and farm support. The government aims to finish in 2026, and a 14th meeting was being prepared in November.

Membership will bind maximum tariff rates and lock in MFN treatment for Uzbek exports to all members, with access to dispute settlement. The unified fee scale and the new non-MFN duty were adopted for this purpose. Further changes to licensing, technical regulation and subsidies should be expected as commitments are finalised.

> Membership will bind maximum tariff rates and lock in MFN treatment for Uzbek exports to all members.

## Free warehouses and free customs zones

In free warehouses and free customs zones, goods are held without duty, taxes or trade policy measures (Customs Code, Arts. 100 and 106); in a zone they may also be used, for example in production. Free warehouses are licensed, and goods in them can change hands by written notice. Duty and VAT fall due only when goods enter the domestic market.

A pilot for e-commerce bonded warehouses runs from 1 July 2026 to 1 July 2028 under a presidential resolution of 13 April 2026. Operators need a free warehouse licence and at least 15,000 square metres of space. Goods are cleared order by order when a customer buys through a registered platform and may stay for up to two years. Free economic zones offer further customs relief.
