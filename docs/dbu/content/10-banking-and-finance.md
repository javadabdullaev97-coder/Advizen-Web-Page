## The banking system

The Central Bank of the Republic of Uzbekistan (CBU) is the issuing bank and the monetary authority. It is independent of the executive and licenses and supervises all banking activity under the Law on Banks and Banking Activity of 5 November 2019. Banks must be joint-stock companies.

On 3 September 2026, the CBU register listed 35 commercial banks: nine state-owned banks, seven other joint-stock banks, 14 private banks and five banks with foreign capital. State banks still dominate the sector. In March 2026 they held 60% of banking capital and 63% of assets. Fitch, Moody's and S&P all rate the sector's outlook as stable.

Privatisation has been slower than planned. Only two of the six planned sales of state banks have been completed. The updated Uzbekistan-2030 strategy of 16 February 2026 rules out any sale of a state bank in 2026. It plans to cut the number of state banks from nine to four by 2030. The EBRD is acquiring a 15% stake in Asakabank before its sale, and 30% of Uzpromstroybank (SQB) has been transferred to the National Investment Fund.

## Currency regulation

### Convertibility and repatriation

Uzbekistan has accepted the obligations of Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Under the Law on Currency Regulation (ZRU-573 of 22 October 2019), current international transactions are free. They include payments for goods and services, interest, dividends and other investment income, and the related purchase of foreign currency (Art. 15). Inbound direct investment and its repatriation are also unrestricted (Art. 16). Other capital transactions, such as cross-border loans and deposits abroad, follow separate procedures.

A company with foreign shareholders can buy foreign currency from its bank to pay dividends once all taxes have been paid. In practice the conversion takes no more than a week. The authorities can stop a repatriation only in cases of insolvency, criminal proceedings or a court or arbitral order.

The CBU sets the official exchange rate daily from trading on the currency market. On 4 November 2026 the rate was UZS 11,911.71 per USD.

:::box The Tashkent International Financial Centre
- Constitutional Law ZRU-1158 of 13 July 2026 created the Tashkent International Financial Centre (TIFC), a separate legal regime with its own regulator and court.
- Inside the centre, national currency control does not apply to participants' currency operations unless a TIFC resolution provides otherwise.
- The centre is not yet operating, and its detailed currency rules are still to be adopted.
:::

### Accounts

Residents may hold foreign currency accounts with Uzbek banks. A resident company may open an account abroad only with a decision of the President or the Government, or under an international treaty. It must then notify the tax authority and the CBU (Art. 12). Non-residents may open accounts in soum and in foreign currency. A foreign legal entity must, however, carry on activity in Uzbekistan, for example through a representative office (Art. 13).

### Payments between residents

All payments for goods and services inside Uzbekistan must be made in soum (Art. 9). Prices may not be pegged to a foreign currency or a conventional unit, except in foreign-investment projects approved at government level. Foreign currency transactions between residents are banned unless they fall within the closed list in Art. 17. The main permitted cases are:

- Settlements between agents or commission agents and their principals under contracts with non-residents.
- Contributions to charter capital and their return.
- Use of foreign currency as collateral.
- Banking services, and factoring where the debtor is a non-resident.
- Business travel expenses, charity and inheritance.

### Repatriation of trade proceeds

Exporters must receive their export proceeds, and importers must receive the goods they have paid for. If the non-resident's obligation is still outstanding 180 days after it arose, the transaction is treated as a capital transaction (Art. 11). Once a further 45 days have passed, or 90 days for small businesses, fines apply under Art. 11-1. The fine is 5% of the unrepatriated amount, rising by a further 10% after 360 days and a further 35% after 545 days.

## Financing and interest rates

The CBU has kept its policy rate at 14% since 24 March 2025. It held the rate again on 3 November 2026, after considering a cut to 13.5%. Inflation was 6.4% in September 2026 against a 5% target, and the CBU expects 7% at the end of 2026. It has said the rate could fall below 10% over 2027 to 2029 if inflation returns to target, but has made no commitment to that path.

Borrowing in soum is expensive. In September 2026 the weighted average rate on soum loans was 20.4%, and 20.8% for legal entities. Foreign currency loans averaged 10.5%. Foreign-owned companies have the same access to local credit, but the terms are rarely competitive. Some banks on-lend credit lines from international financial institutions. Loans from foreign shareholders or lenders are capital transactions under the currency rules.

> Borrowing in soum is expensive: in September 2026 companies paid 20.8% on average.

## Capital markets

The Republican Stock Exchange Tashkent (UZSE) is the main trading venue. Trades are settled by the National Clearing Centre. Turnover in 2025 was about USD 673 million, and the UCI index rose 10.2% to 721.73 points. The market is still developing, and a comprehensive capital markets law remains in draft. The National Agency for Perspective Projects (NAPP) regulates the capital market.

The privatisation programme now favours selling minority stakes on the market. The Uzbekistan National Investment Fund (UzNIF), managed by Franklin Templeton, holds minority stakes in 13 state companies. In May 2026 it completed a dual IPO, with global depositary receipts in London and ordinary shares in Tashkent. The offering raised USD 603.6 million before over-allotment. It was the first international equity offering by an Uzbek issuer.

The state borrows abroad mainly in its own currency. On 1 April 2026 it placed a three-year international soum bond worth UZS 12.2 trillion (USD 1 billion equivalent) at 12.25%. Orders totalled UZS 23.4 trillion. Comparable issues priced at 16.625% in 2024 and 15.5% in 2025. Presidential Resolution PP-359 of 27 November 2025 allows resident companies, from 1 January 2026, to issue tokenised shares and bonds on separate platforms of licensed exchanges, under rules to be set by NAPP.

## Islamic finance

Islamic finance first reached the market through microfinance organisations. Since 2024 they have been able to offer six products under CBU Resolution No. 23/4 of 19 July 2024: murabaha, ijara, ijara muntahiya bittamlik, salam, mudaraba and musharaka. Financing is capped at UZS 300 million for businesses and UZS 100 million for individuals. Each provider needs a Sharia council of at least five members.

Law ZRU-1126 of 27 March 2026 introduced Islamic banking from the end of June 2026. It amended the Civil Code, the Tax Code, the Law on the Central Bank and the Law on Banks. Full Islamic banks and Islamic windows in conventional banks are both permitted. An Islamic Finance Council now sits within the CBU. Open Bank received the first Islamic banking licence in October 2026, and more than ten banks are preparing Islamic windows. The regulator expects at least ten Islamic banks by 2030.

## Crypto-assets

Crypto-assets are not legal tender and may not be used as a means of payment unless the law allows it. Crypto businesses need a licence from NAPP under Regulation No. 3380 of 15 August 2022. NAPP issues four licence types, each held separately. Only Uzbek-resident legal entities can hold a licence, so a foreign group must set up a local company. Licences have no time limit and cannot be transferred. Servers must be in Uzbekistan, records must be kept for five years, and no offshore company may appear in the ownership chain.

:::table NAPP licence fees, in BCV
| Licence | One-off state duty | Monthly levy |
|---|---|---|
| Crypto exchange | 73,400 (~USD 2.7 m) | 740 (~USD 27,000) |
| Crypto depository | 7,000 (~USD 260,000) | 5 (~USD 180) |
| Crypto shop | 3,700 (~USD 140,000) | 185 (~USD 6,800) |
| Mining pool | 3,000 (~USD 110,000) | 100 (~USD 3,700) |
:::

A crypto exchange needs charter capital of at least 5,000 BCV (~USD 180,000), of which 3,000 BCV (~USD 110,000) must be held in a reserve account with an Uzbek bank. NAPP must decide on an application within 20 working days. Since 16 April 2026, NAPP and the CBU have run a regulatory sandbox for payments in stablecoins (joint regulation No. 3818).

## Payment systems

Domestic card payments run on two national systems, Humo and Uzcard. Local banks also issue Visa and Mastercard cards, which are needed for most payments abroad.

Amendments to the Law on Personal Data took effect on 27 March 2026. They ended the rule that all personal data had to be stored on servers in Uzbekistan. Biometric and genetic data, and data on users of local telecom operators, must still stay in the country. Other personal data may be stored and processed abroad if one of three conditions is met:

- The destination country offers equivalent protection.
- The operator uses approved standard contractual clauses or binding corporate rules.
- The operator follows a recognised international standard.

The change cleared the way for global wallets. Apple Pay and Google Pay went live on 27 October 2026 for Visa and Mastercard cards of eight and nine local banks respectively. The CBU says Humo and Uzcard cards will follow but has given no date.
