## Personal data

The Law "On Personal Data" was substantially amended by Law No. ZRU-1125 of 26 March 2026, in force since 27 March 2026. The amendments abandoned the general requirement to store the personal data of Uzbek citizens on servers in Uzbekistan. The change was made largely to let international payment platforms and e-commerce services operate legally.

### Localisation now limited to listed categories

Only three categories must still be stored and processed on servers in Uzbekistan:

- biometric data, such as fingerprints, facial images, iris data and voice samples;
- genetic data, including DNA profiles and genetic test results;
- personal data of users of telecommunications services provided by operators in Uzbekistan.

Databases containing these categories must be entered in the State Register of Personal Data Databases.

### Cross-border transfer

Other personal data may be stored and processed abroad if one of three conditions is met. The destination country is on the Government's list of countries with adequate protection; or the operator uses standard contractual clauses or binding corporate rules that meet requirements set by the authorised state body; or the operator complies with international data protection standards approved by that body. The requirements for contractual clauses and corporate rules are still being developed. Under the Government resolution described below, incidents involving data transferred abroad must be notified to the authorised body within 24 hours, with a report on causes and remedies within 72 hours.

### The list of 49 countries

Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 415 of 29 July 2026, effective 3 August 2026, approved a list of 49 countries and territories that provide an adequate level of protection. It includes the EU member states, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, Canada, Japan, the Republic of Korea, Singapore, New Zealand, Israel, the Russian Federation, Brazil, Argentina and Hong Kong. The United States is listed only for companies operating within the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Listing does not remove the operator's other obligations; each transfer arrangement still needs to be assessed.

### Liability for unlawful processing with AI

Law No. ZRU-1115 of 21 January 2026, in force on publication, made it an administrative offence to process personal data unlawfully using artificial intelligence, or to spread such data through the media, telecoms networks or the internet. The fine is 50 to 100 BCV (~USD 1,800 to 3,700), and the tools used may be confiscated. The same law prohibits legally significant decisions affecting individuals' rights from being based solely on the output of an AI system.

:::box Key points for data controllers
- Map which data is biometric, genetic or telecoms user data: only these must stay in Uzbekistan.
- Check whether the recipient country is on the list of 49.
- Otherwise put contractual clauses or binding corporate rules in place once the requirements are published.
- Review AI tools that process personal data against the 2026 liability rules.
:::

## Intellectual property

The Ministry of Justice is the national intellectual property office; applications are handled by its Intellectual Property Centre, a state institution. Uzbekistan is a member of WIPO and of the Madrid system for trademarks and the Patent Cooperation Treaty for patents. Trademarks, patents, utility models, industrial designs, software and databases can all be registered.

A trademark is registered for 10 years from the filing date and can be renewed for further 10-year periods. A mark not used for three years without good reason can be cancelled at the request of a third party. Law No. ZRU-1144 of 7 May 2026, in force since 8 August 2026, ended the rule that a licence agreement in itself counts as use of the mark: owners must now show genuine commercial use. Early termination of a trademark must now be sought in court rather than before the Ministry's Board of Appeal. Official fees are set in BCV and rose by about 6.8% on 1 September 2026, when the BCV was increased.

Rights holders can also record their trademarks and other rights in the customs register, so that customs can suspend the release of suspected counterfeit goods.

## Contracts and choice of law

Commercial contracts are governed by the Civil Code. Parties to a contract with a foreign element are free to choose the law of any country, for the whole contract or for parts of it. Where disputes will be heard in Uzbekistan, Uzbek law is often the more practical choice, because a local court or tribunal applies it with more confidence. Payments between Uzbek residents must generally be made in soums, while cross-border contracts may be priced and paid in any currency.

Joint-venture partners in an LLC may sign a corporate agreement under Article 358-1 of the Civil Code. It binds only its parties, and clauses that require participants to vote as the company's bodies instruct are void. Inside the Tashkent International Financial Centre, contracts between participants can be governed by TIFC law, which follows English common law where its own rules are silent.

## Dispute resolution

### State economic courts

Commercial disputes between businesses are heard by the economic courts under the Economic Procedure Code, with appeals to the Supreme Court. A specialised panel of the Supreme Court hears certain investment disputes involving investments of at least USD 20 million, as well as competition cases.

### Tashkent International Arbitration Centre

International arbitration is governed by Law No. ZRU-674 "On International Commercial Arbitration" of 16 February 2021, based on the UNCITRAL Model Law. The Tashkent International Arbitration Centre (TIAC) was established in 2018 under the Chamber of Commerce and Industry and has operated since 2019. It charges no administrative fees and runs largely virtual proceedings. TIAC and the Hong Kong International Arbitration Centre offer joint cross-institutional rules.

Domestic arbitration is governed by a separate law, amended from 24 July 2026 by Law No. ZRU-1141. Under the amendments, land and building ownership disputes cannot be referred to domestic arbitration, and state bodies and enterprises with state participation cannot be parties to domestic arbitration agreements.

> Uzbekistan acceded to the New York Convention in 1996 without reciprocity or commercial reservations.

### Enforcement of foreign arbitral awards

Uzbekistan acceded to the New York Convention in 1996 without reciprocity or commercial reservations. A creditor applies to the economic court where the debtor is located, with the award, the arbitration agreement and a certified Uzbek translation. The court must decide within six months. The grounds for refusal mirror Article V of the Convention, and the courts read public policy narrowly. A Supreme Court plenary resolution of 20 November 2023 guides enforcement practice.

## Land and real estate

All land is owned by the state unless it has been privatised. Foreign citizens, foreign legal entities and enterprises with foreign investments may hold land only on lease (Land Code, Art. 17). Their leases are granted by the Cabinet of Ministers, for up to 25 years, against a payment linked to the market value of the lease right (Art. 24). Leases of non-agricultural land for others can run for up to 100 years, and the tenant has a pre-emptive right to renew.

Uzbek citizens and legal entities may buy non-agricultural plots through privatisation under Law No. ZRU-728 of 15 November 2021, usually by electronic auction. Enterprises with foreign investments and foreign persons are excluded (Art. 13), and a plot that passes to them must be sold within six months (Art. 33). Foreign investors may own buildings and premises, but hold the land beneath them on lease; the only exception concerns diplomatic missions (Land Code, Arts. 17 and 18).

Access to farmland was tightened from 1 May 2026. Foreign persons and enterprises with foreign participation may no longer lease agricultural land directly, bid at auctions or acquire it by sub-lease. For projects above USD 10 million, regional directorates take the land on a 25-year lease and sub-lease it to the investor.

## Competition and merger control

The Law "On Competition" (No. ZRU-850 of 3 July 2023) is enforced by the Competition Promotion and Consumer Protection Committee. Prior consent is required for a merger or acquisition if either test is met:

- the parties' combined balance-sheet assets or sales in the last calendar year exceed 500,000 BCV (~USD 18 million);
- the assets or sales of one party exceed 250,000 BCV (~USD 9 million).

Acquisitions of more than 25% of the voting shares of a JSC, or more than one third of the charter capital of an LLC, are caught (Art. 26). The Committee decides within 30 calendar days, extendable by up to two months. The consent fee is 0.05% of the parties' sales, with a minimum of 7 BCV (~USD 260) and a maximum of 1,000 BCV (~USD 37,000). Closing without consent carries a fine of 1,000 BCV, and the Committee may ask a court to invalidate the deal.

## Protection of investments

The Law on Investments and Investment Activities (No. ZRU-598 of 25 December 2019) protects foreign investors against adverse changes in law. If later legislation worsens the conditions of investment, the law in force on the investment date applies to the foreign investor for 10 years (Art. 19). The guarantee covers new repatriation hurdles, investment volume limits, restrictions on foreign shareholdings and new visa procedures. It does not cover national security legislation.

Investments may not be nationalised. They may be requisitioned only in natural disasters, epidemics and other emergencies, with adequate compensation (Art. 21). Investors may freely repatriate income after tax and may recover losses caused by unlawful acts of state bodies.

Uzbekistan has been party to the ICSID Convention since 25 August 1995 and has about 50 bilateral investment treaties in force. Recent treaties include India (in force since 15 May 2025) and Italy and Jordan (both signed in 2025). The Law on Investments allows investment disputes to go to international arbitration where a treaty or a contract with the state provides valid consent.
