### Reading the table

The rates are percentages of the gross payment. A treaty rate applies only if the recipient is the beneficial owner of the income and provides a certificate of tax residence. Where a treaty rate is higher than the domestic rate, the domestic rate applies: 10% on dividends and interest and 20% on royalties (Tax Code, Art. 353). Most treaties also exempt interest paid to governments, central banks and state export credit agencies. Those exemptions are not shown in the table.

The Japan treaty entered into force on 17 October 2020, applies to Uzbek withholding tax from 1 January 2021 and contains a limitation-on-benefits article (Art. 28). A Protocol to the Finland treaty, in force since 3 July 2016, rewrote the exchange-of-information article and added assistance in tax collection; it left the rates unchanged. The Malaysia treaty entered into force on 10 August 1999 and applies to withholding tax from 1 January 2000. The Qatar treaty entered into force on 12 February 2024.

### Notes to the table

:::notes
- (a) The lower rate applies if the recipient is a company that holds at least 10% of the paying company's capital.
- (b) The lower rate applies if the recipient is a company that controls at least 10% of the voting power of the paying company.
- (c) The lower rate applies to a company holding at least 20% (for Poland, at least 20% of the voting power).
- (d) The lower rate applies to a company holding at least 25% of the paying company's capital.
- (e) The lower rate applies to a company holding at least 25% (capital for Egypt, voting power for Japan) throughout a 365-day period that includes the payment date (for Japan, the date on which entitlement to the dividends is determined). Under the Japan treaty, dividends that the payer can deduct are taxed at up to 10%.
- (f) 0% on interest on commercial credit, that is, deferred payment for goods or services supplied by an enterprise. The Austrian treaty says this interest may be exempted.
- (g) 0% on interest on credit sales of goods or equipment and on bank loans.
- (h) 0% on interest on bank loans.
- (i) 0% on interest on credit sales of goods or equipment between unrelated enterprises and on government-supported export credits.
- (j) 10% on interest received by financial institutions, including insurance companies; 15% in other cases.
- (k) 5% on copyright royalties for literary, dramatic, musical or artistic works (films excluded) and on royalties for software, patents and know-how; 10% on other royalties.
- (l) 0% on royalties for software, patents, designs, models and plans; 5% for secret formulas, processes and know-how; 10% for copyright, including films, and trademarks.
- (m) 3% on royalties for patents, trademarks, designs, models, plans, secret formulas, know-how and copyright in scientific works; 5% for copyright in literary and artistic works, including films.
- (n) 5% on copyright royalties for literary, artistic or scientific works, films excluded; 10% on other royalties.
- (o) 0% on copyright royalties for literary, artistic or scientific works, including films; 5% on other royalties.
- (p) 2% on payments for the use of industrial, commercial or scientific equipment; 5% on other royalties.
- (q) Under the most-favoured-nation clause (Protocol, point 4), lower rates that Uzbekistan agrees with an OECD member state apply to France automatically. Through the Slovenia treaty, the 10% rate on other dividends fell to 8% from 1 January 2014 (BOI-INT-CVB-UZB-20, § 110).
- (r) Under the Protocol (items VII and X), the 5% dividend rate falls to 0% while a Dutch company pays no Dutch company tax on dividends from an Uzbek company. Interest and royalty rates fall to 0% while the Netherlands levies no withholding tax on such payments to Uzbek residents. The English text prevails over the Uzbek one.
- (s) Under the Protocol, dividends that qualify for the 5% rate are taxed at 0% while Spanish corporate tax law exempts such dividends received by a Spanish company.
:::
