The tax system at a glance

Moderate flat rates, strict paperwork

Corporate income tax

Who pays, on what and at which rate

Who pays

Resident companies pay corporate income tax on worldwide income. Permanent establishments of foreign companies pay it on income connected with their activity in Uzbekistan. Individual entrepreneurs above the turnover threshold, or those who opt in, pay it as well.

Taxable income

The base is total income less deductible expenses. An expense is deductible when it is economically justified and supported by documents, and the standard of documentation is high. Most disputes with the tax authority are about the paper behind a deduction, not about the rate.

Rates

  • 15% general rate, also for permanent establishments
  • 20% for banks, mobile operators and owners of markets and shopping centres
  • 0% for farming of own produce, the social sector, small renewable generation and, for three years from commissioning, products made with high technologies (Art. 337)
  • E-commerce companies pay the general 15% instead of 10% since 1 January 2026
  • A company whose income first exceeds UZS 10 billion may halve its rate for that year and the next while income stays below UZS 100 billion
  • A company leaving turnover tax for the first time from 2026 is exempt for one year, except on dividends and interest

Tax period and payments

The tax year is the calendar year and the reporting period is the quarter. Profit is calculated on a cumulative basis, so a loss in one quarter reduces the tax for the next. Quarterly returns and payments are due by the 20th of the month after the quarter and the annual return by 1 March. Companies with prior-year income above UZS 20 billion also pay monthly advances by the 23rd (Art. 340).

Corporate income tax

Depreciation, losses and interest

Depreciation

Fixed assets are depreciated on a straight-line basis at rates no higher than these. A company may choose lower rates.

Asset groupMaximum rate
Buildings5%
Structures, aircraft, ships, rolling stock10%
Pipelines, power and telecom lines15%
Machinery and equipment, cars and trucks20%
Computers and peripherals40%
Other assets15%

Tax Code, Art. 306. Land and construction in progress are not depreciated. Intangibles are amortised over their useful life, or five years when it cannot be determined.

Losses

A loss can be carried forward without a time limit and without a cap on the share of profit it offsets. Losses are used in the order they arose, only against the result of a full tax year. A loss on the sale of a shareholding or a business can be set only against gains on similar assets (Art. 336).

Thin capitalisation

Interest on debt owed to a foreign shareholder with more than 20%, or to its related parties, is limited once that debt exceeds three times the company's equity, thirteen times for banks and leasing companies. Third-party loans guaranteed by the shareholder are caught too (Art. 310).

Investment deduction

20% of the cost of new technological equipment, modernisation and domestic software, and 10% of spending on new production buildings, can be deducted in the period the asset is put into use. The deduction is reversed if the asset is sold within three years (Art. 308).

Cross-border payments

Withholding tax, treaties and permanent establishment

Withholding tax

  • 10% on dividends, interest and insurance premiums
  • 6% on international freight and telecoms
  • 20% on all other income, including service fees
  • 0% on interest on loans from foreign financial institutions that fund investment projects (Art. 353)
  • Management, technical and consulting fees are Uzbek-source income even when the work is done abroad (Art. 351)
  • The tax is paid by the day after the payment and reported by the 20th of the next month

Treaties

  • 55 double tax treaties in force. Most reduce withholding on dividends, interest and royalties
  • The treaty rate applies only with a certificate of tax residence given before the payment, apostilled or printed from the foreign tax authority's website (Art. 358)
  • For dividends, interest and royalties the recipient must be the beneficial owner
  • Tax withheld for lack of a certificate can be reclaimed later

Permanent establishment

A fixed place of business creates one, as does a building site or services through staff for more than 183 days in any twelve months, with related companies added together (Art. 36). Profit left at its disposal is taxed like a dividend at 10%, unless a treaty sets a lower rate.

Controlled foreign companies

An Uzbek resident that controls a foreign company includes the company's retained profit in its own taxable income. Control starts at a holding of more than 25%, or more than 10% when Uzbek residents together hold more than half.

The profit is exempt for active companies outside the official offshore list, and for treaty-country banks, insurers and companies paying an effective rate of at least 15%. Profit of UZS 300 million or less is ignored. Controlled companies are reported by 20 March of the following year (Arts. 204, 208, 209).

Value added tax

Registration, rates and invoices

Who is a VAT payer

VAT is charged at 12% on supplies of goods and services in Uzbekistan and on imports. Every Uzbek company is a VAT payer unless it uses turnover tax, which since 1 June 2026 is possible up to annual income of 12,000 base calculation values (Decree UP-100). Above that level VAT applies from the day the threshold is crossed.

Registration is made in the taxpayer's online account, and voluntary registration is possible. Returns are monthly, filed and paid by the 20th (Arts. 259, 273).

Rates

  • 12% standard rate
  • 0% on exports, international transport and other listed supplies (Art. 258)
  • 6% of turnover, without input VAT, as an option for retail, catering and services from 1 June 2026

Exemptions

Education, healthcare, financial services and a long list of social goods are exempt, and input VAT on exempt activity cannot be recovered. A zero-rated exporter keeps the right to recover input VAT but must prove the export with customs and payment documents. Since 2026 sales by listed agricultural producers, except cotton and grain, are zero-rated too.

Input VAT and e-invoices

Input VAT is deducted only on an electronic invoice from the state e-invoicing system. Since 1 January 2026 an automated system rates every invoice by risk, and VAT on a high-risk invoice is credited only once the supplier, or the buyer as tax agent, has paid it.

Value added tax

Services from abroad, digital sellers and refunds

Services from abroad

When a foreign company supplies services to an Uzbek business, the Uzbek business pays the VAT as tax agent and may then deduct it. If the price excludes VAT, the buyer adds 12% and pays it from its own funds, whether or not it is itself a VAT payer (Art. 255).

As a rule a service is supplied where the buyer is established, so most services bought by an Uzbek company are taxed in Uzbekistan. Services tied to real estate or performed on site follow their own rules (Art. 241).

Refunds

Excess input VAT is carried forward or refunded. Since 1 June 2026 a balance of up to UZS 10 million is repaid to small businesses without an application or audit. Larger refunds are paid within 30 days of a desk audit (Art. 274). Large taxpayers, established exporters and those giving a bank guarantee are refunded within seven days, and businesses rated AAA within one day.

Foreign digital sellers

Foreign providers of digital services to consumers register and pay VAT directly through the tax portal. They register within 30 days of the first sale and file and pay quarterly by the 20th of the following month (Arts. 279 to 281). When the customer is an Uzbek company, the company pays as tax agent.

Goods sold online from 12 December 2026

  • Foreign sellers of goods to consumers in Uzbekistan through electronic platforms become VAT payers
  • Uzbek platforms that settle with foreign sellers pay the VAT as tax agents
  • Direct sellers register within 30 calendar days of the first sale
  • Law ZRU-1173 of 10 September 2026

Small business

Turnover tax and the simplified 6% VAT

The reform of 1 June 2026 raised the turnover tax threshold and added a new 6% VAT option for retail, catering and services.

Turnover tax

Turnover tax replaces profit tax and VAT. Since 1 June 2026 a company may use it while annual income stays below 12,000 base calculation values, instead of UZS 1 billion (Decree UP-100 of 26 May 2026). Turnover tax payers still pay social tax and property, land and water taxes. Returns are monthly by the 15th, with an annual return by 15 February (Art. 470).

TaxpayerRate
All sectors, unless listed below4%
Retail in cities of 100,000 people or more4%
Retail in other settlements2%
Retail in remote and mountain districts1%
Individual entrepreneurs and the self-employed1%

Tax Code, Art. 467. Importers, producers of excisable goods, mining companies, tax consultants, auditors and others cannot use it (Art. 461).

The simplified 6% VAT

From 1 June 2026 to 1 January 2030, a business whose main activity is retail, catering or services may pay VAT at 6% of total turnover instead of 12% on value added. Its profit tax rate is then 0%, but it cannot deduct input VAT and loses refunds. Its customers can still deduct the 6%. Large taxpayers, state-owned enterprises and manufacturers cannot use it.

Choosing between 6% and 12%

  • 6% suits high-margin businesses with few taxable inputs
  • Businesses with large VAT-bearing purchases usually pay less at 12%
  • Exporters lose the zero rate and refunds under 6%

Payroll taxes

Personal income tax and social tax

  • Residency: more than 183 days in Uzbekistan in any twelve months beginning or ending in the tax year (Art. 30). Residents are taxed on worldwide income
  • 12% flat personal income tax for residents. Non-residents pay 12% on employment income and 10% on dividends and interest (Arts. 381, 382)
  • 5% on dividends and interest of residents, withheld by the payer. Bank deposit interest is exempt
  • 12% social tax paid by the employer on top of gross payroll (Art. 405)
  • Returns and payments monthly by the 15th, annual employer report by 15 February
  • 0.1% of salary goes to an individual pension account and is deducted from the income tax withheld (Art. 385)

Foreign employees

Foreign employees of an Uzbek company are taxed like local staff. When a foreign company seconds staff under a service contract, the Uzbek recipient pays social tax on their pay, and at least on 90% of the contract value (Art. 404).

Since 1 January 2026 foreign citizens who pay a fixed fee, own or rent housing in Uzbekistan and spend more than 30 days a year there may have their foreign-source income exempted for up to five years (Decree UP-180).

Other taxes

Property, land, water, subsoil and excise

  • Property tax 1.5% of the average net book value of real estate, but not less than a minimum value per square metre (Arts. 412, 415). 3% for construction left unfinished beyond its term, 0.7% for railways, trunk pipelines and power lines
  • Land tax is a fixed amount per hectare set by region and adjusted locally. Rent for state land counts as land tax (Art. 429)
  • Water use tax per cubic metre, five times the rate above the permitted limit (Art. 445)
  • Subsoil use tax as a share of the value extracted, for example 10% for oil and gas, 7% for gold and copper, 4% for coal (Art. 452)

Excise

Due on the production and import of tobacco, alcohol, fuel, natural gas, polyethylene granules, sugary and energy drinks and crisps. Rates are fixed per unit or a share of value, and the return is due monthly by the 10th (Art. 292).

Property tax returns are annual by 1 March, with monthly advances by the 10th.

Transfer pricing

Related-party transactions under control

Controlled transactions

The rules cover transactions between related parties, foreign trade in exchange-traded commodities such as metals, fertilisers, oil products and cotton, and any deal with a party in a listed offshore jurisdiction (Arts. 180, 181).

Thresholds

Cross-border related-party transactions are controlled whatever their size. Deals between related Uzbek residents are controlled above UZS 5 billion a year, or UZS 500 million when one side has a special regime or reduced rate.

Documentation

Controlled transactions are notified by the deadline for annual financial statements (Art. 182). Documentation can be requested from 1 June of the following year and must be provided within 30 days (Arts. 193, 195). Advance pricing agreements are available.

Automated control

In May 2026 the Tax Committee announced an automated system that identifies related parties, tests prices against the market and flags risks. Expect questions on related-party prices before any audit starts.

Global minimum tax (Pillar Two)

  • Uzbekistan joined the OECD Inclusive Framework in 2023 but has not introduced the 15% minimum tax
  • The rules apply to groups with revenue of EUR 750 million or more
  • At 15%, the standard rate meets the minimum
  • Subsidiaries on 0% rates or incentives may trigger top-up tax abroad

Tax administration

Registration, audits, penalties and appeals

Registration and e-invoicing

A company is registered for tax automatically on incorporation. A foreign company with a permanent establishment registers within 30 days if its contract exceeds 183 days, and otherwise within 183 days (Art. 130). Invoices are issued only through the state e-invoicing system, and since 2026 the authority pre-fills VAT, turnover tax and payroll returns.

Audits

Desk audits review returns. Full tax audits target high-risk taxpayers, are announced 30 days ahead and last 30 days, extendable to six months (Arts. 137 to 142). Tax can be reassessed for three years after the end of the period (Art. 88). Businesses rated AAA are not audited, except in criminal cases.

Penalties

  • 20% of underpaid tax
  • 20% of a concealed tax base
  • 40% of the tax on non-market transfer pricing (Arts. 223 to 226)
  • Halved for prompt payment with admission
  • Late payment interest of 1/300 of the Central Bank refinancing rate per day

Appeals

Audit decisions can be appealed to the higher tax authority within one month, and the appeal suspends collection (Arts. 231, 232). The taxpayer may also go directly to court. If a decision is cancelled, tax and penalties are returned with interest.
