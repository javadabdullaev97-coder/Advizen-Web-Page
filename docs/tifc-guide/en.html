At a glance

A new legal order inside Tashkent

Decree UP-48 of 30 March 2026 created the Tashkent International Financial Centre (TIFC), and Constitutional Law ZRU-1158 of 13 July 2026 gave it its legal regime. The centre is expected to start operating in 2027.

TIFC is a defined territory with its own regulator, its own court and its own law. Inside it, the decisions of the centre's bodies prevail over conflicting national law, and English common law and equity fill the gaps.

The centre is open to banks, asset managers, insurers, market infrastructure, Islamic finance, digital-asset businesses, holding companies and the professional firms that serve them.

Qualified participants are exempt from profit tax and social tax on income from financial services until 1 January 2076. Foreign staff need no work permit, and national currency control does not apply to operations inside the centre.

This guide explains what the law already fixes, what is still to be written in the centre's resolutions, and how a business can prepare for activation.

What TIFC is

Not another special economic zone

A special economic zone grants tax and customs relief inside the national legal system. TIFC replaces the legal system itself for the business done inside it.

Legal architecture

Which law applies inside the centre

Article 8 of the law sets the order of precedence. Where a higher source answers a question, the lower ones do not apply.

  1. ConstitutionThe Constitution of the Republic of Uzbekistan
  2. ZRU-1158The Constitutional Law on the centre
  3. Presidential actsDecrees and resolutions of the President on the centre
  4. TIFC resolutionsActs of the centre's bodies, called TIFC decisions
  5. English lawPrinciples of common law and equity of England and Wales where nothing else applies

Primacy inside the territory

Within the centre, TIFC decisions prevail over any conflicting national law. The TIFC Council decides which English legislation applies, as of which date and with what changes. Common law and equity then fill the remaining gaps.

National law that still applies

  • The Constitution and criminal law
  • A limited part of administrative liability
  • National security, defence and anti-money-laundering rules
  • Immigration, family, environmental, public health and emergency law
  • International treaties and any national law a TIFC resolution adopts

Article 12 of Constitutional Law ZRU-1158. Everything outside these areas is governed by the TIFC framework.

Common law

What changes for your contracts

Uzbekistan is a civil-law country with codified private law. Inside TIFC, two participants can sign under TIFC law and litigate in an English-language court on terms familiar from London, Dubai or Astana.

  • 01

    Precedent

    Outcomes follow a long line of decided cases on standard commercial terms, so lenders and funds can price legal risk.

  • 02

    Freedom of contract

    Courts enforce the bargain as written. Civil codes more often impose mandatory rules over what the parties agreed.

  • 03

    Trusts and equity

    Trusts, specific performance and injunctions have no direct equivalent in Uzbek civil law. Funds and security structures rely on them.

  • 04

    Interpretation

    Contracts are read by their words and commercial purpose, which is how international finance and M&A documents are drafted.

  • 05

    Remedies

    Equitable relief, freezing orders and settled measures of damages come from the common-law tradition.

  • 06

    One forum

    Parties no longer need to choose a foreign law and a foreign court and then enforce the result in Uzbekistan.

Who can operate

Activities open in the centre

Article 7 covers almost the whole range of financial services and the functions around them.

Banking and markets

  • Deposits, lending and project finance
  • Trading in financial instruments, currencies, commodities and derivatives
  • Brokerage, market making and payment services

Asset management and Islamic finance

  • Fund, portfolio, pension and hedge fund management
  • Securities issuance and trading, crowdfunding, financial advice
  • Sharia-compliant banking, investment and capital markets

Insurance and infrastructure

  • Insurance and reinsurance, underwriting and brokerage
  • Custody, clearing, settlement and depositories
  • Stock and commodity exchanges, alternative trading systems

Digital assets and support

  • Issuance, trading and custody of digital assets, digital-asset exchanges and funds
  • Audit, accounting, legal and rating services
  • Holding companies, special purpose vehicles, treasury companies and representative offices

Still to be defined

Which of these count as qualifying financial services for the tax exemption, and the licensing rules for each, will be set by TIFC resolutions and the Financial Services Authority.

Tax

The 2076 exemption and its limits

The Tax Code applies inside the centre only where the Constitutional Law or a TIFC resolution expressly says so (Article 18).

Standard Uzbek LLCQualified TIFC participant
Profit tax15%0% on income from financial services until 1 January 2076
Social tax12% of payroll0% until 2076 on qualifying activity
Dividends, VAT and personal income tax10% withholding on dividends to non-residents, 5% to residents, 12% VAT, 12% personal income taxSet by TIFC resolutions, not yet adopted
ConditionsNone beyond the Tax CodeEconomic substance, governance and anti-money-laundering compliance
Groups with revenue of EUR 750m or moreStandard rates and the Pillar Two rulesNo exemption. A qualified domestic minimum top-up tax at the standard 15% rate

TIFC bodies

The centre's bodies and the entities they wholly own are exempt from profit tax and social tax until 1 January 2076.

The Pillar Two test

A participant loses the exemption if its group had consolidated revenue of EUR 750 million or more in at least two of the four preceding years (Article 19).

In practice

For a mid-sized financial services business the regime is a fifty-year holiday. For a large international group it is closer to the standard Uzbek rate, with the legal and operational advantages of the centre.

Operating in the centre

People, money and assets

Foreign staff and visas

  • No work permit and no quota for qualified foreign staff whose main workplace is the centre (Article 21)
  • The TIFC Administration sets the qualification requirements and keeps the register of foreign staff
  • Entry visa at an Uzbek embassy or on arrival at an international airport (Article 20)
  • Visas of up to five years for persons who qualify as TIFC persons

Currency and assets

  • Obligations between participants can be set and settled in foreign currency, and in digital assets within the limits of the centre's rules (Article 17)
  • No registration of currency contracts, no notices on capital movements and no limits on foreign accounts, unless a TIFC resolution agreed with the Central Bank says otherwise
  • No confiscation, nationalisation or freezing of participants' assets except by order of the centre's court or under AML, sanctions, criminal or insolvency law (Article 6)

Commercial disputes stay commercial

Conduct that is in substance a civil or commercial dispute is handled first through the civil and regulatory tools of the centre, not referred to national authorities for criminal prosecution (Article 14).

Institutions

Who runs the centre

Article 22 creates four bodies, each financially and operationally independent. A Governor appointed by Presidential decree coordinates them and the link with the state.

  • 01

    TIFC Council

    At least five members. Sets strategy, approves budgets and adopts TIFC resolutions.

  • 02

    TIFC Administration

    The executive body that registers participants, runs the centre and promotes it.

  • 03

    Financial Services Authority

    The independent regulator. Licenses, supervises and enforces in financial and ancillary services.

  • 04

    International Commercial Court

    Hears disputes in English (Article 30). It also serves the IT Park.

Investment tax residency

Article 13 creates the status of TIFC investment tax resident for individuals who make qualifying investments in Uzbekistan, and for their family members. The TIFC Council will set the forms, amounts and instruments of investment.

  • Not a tax resident of Uzbekistan in the three years before applying
  • Uzbek citizenship not terminated in the ten years before applying
  • Status may be refused where a main purpose of the application is improper

Each body publishes an annual transparency report within four months of the end of the financial year. Investment thresholds for the residency programme are not yet published.

Timeline

The road to activation

The centre opens once its founding resolutions are adopted and the President approves activation.

  1. March 2026
    Centre createdDecree UP-48 of 30 March 2026 creates the centre and the Tashkent International Commercial Court
  2. July 2026
    Law signed and in forceZRU-1158 of 13 July, in force since 25 July. Article 15 of the Constitution amended
  3. September 2026
    Governor appointedBy Presidential decree of 10 September 2026
  4. Within 12 months
    Founding resolutionsGovernance, regulation, the court and daily operation. Extendable by up to six months (Article 31)
  5. Expected 2027
    ActivationThe Council submits the activation decision to the President. Officials expect the centre to start in 2027
  6. After activation
    Transitional routesUzbek licensees register a subsidiary or branch within 60 days (Article 32). Foreign licensees get a transitional licence where a cooperation agreement exists (Article 33)
