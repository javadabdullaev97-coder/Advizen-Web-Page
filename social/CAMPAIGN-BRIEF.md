# Campaign brief — Q4 2026 launch

`social/PLAN.md` run through the `marketing:campaign-plan` framework
(objective, audience, message, channel, measure). The plan supplies the
editorial judgement; this file supplies the parts a campaign brief is
supposed to carry and the plan does not — a measurable objective, an asset
inventory, targets, risks, and the order of operations before 1 October.

Where the plan already settles something, this brief points at it rather
than restating it. Where the two disagree, the plan wins on editorial
questions and this file flags the conflict.

**Reviewed 21 September 2026.** Ten days to the first post. The 20
September date for Phase 0 has passed with all three blocking items still
open, and nothing in the repository has moved since the first pass — no
publishing workflow, the same three drafts. The production calendar below
is re-anchored to the week of 21 September; risk 6 is now the acute one.

---

## 1. Overview

**Name.** Первые три месяца.

**Summary.** Establish four empty social accounts as the visible surface of
a full-cycle advisory practice in Tashkent, using twenty-six topics from
1 October to 22 December 2026.

**Primary objective.** From a standing start, reach 22 December with four
live accounts, twenty-six topics published without a missed slot, and at
least one inbound enquiry that names a post.

That last clause is the only hard conversion target the plan admits, and
the plan is right to hold it there: on a zero baseline every other number
is a rate of change against nothing.

**Secondary objectives.**

- A grid a stranger can read: by 13 October, six Instagram posts covering
  all four disciplines, so the practice is legible before a word of
  self-description.
- A LinkedIn page that survives inspection by a foreign investor's counsel
  — thirteen long-form English pieces by year end.
- Two to three topics identified by engagement as candidates for full
  articles on the site.

**Stage.** Awareness moving into consideration. Not conversion — the plan
is explicit that a funnel built at zero followers optimises an empty room.

## 2. Audience

Segments, needs and channel fit are set out in `PLAN.md` § Audience and are
not duplicated here. Three things that section leaves implicit and the
brief needs:

**Buying stage differs by channel, and that is the whole reason the four
cuts diverge.** The Instagram reader does not yet know the mechanism is a
risk — awareness. The LinkedIn reader has the problem and is looking for
someone who has seen it before — consideration. Facebook sits between the
two and carries the only link. Twitter is pure awareness among peers, which
is why a Saturday note costs one sentence and no card.

**Primary segment**, in the plan's own terms: a Russian-speaking founder or
finance director in Tashkent who has an international structure question,
does not know which of the four disciplines it belongs to, and discovers
advisers by reputation rather than search.

**Secondary segment**: foreign counsel and corporate development staff
evaluating Uzbekistan, who know corporate law in general and this
jurisdiction not at all.

**Excluded by design.** The even split between practical and global
material means roughly half the December audience will never be clients.
The plan states this as an accepted cost; it is worth restating here so
that nobody reads December's reach figures as a signal to do more of it.

## 3. Key messages

**Core message.** Advizen knows how this jurisdiction actually works, and
will show you rather than tell you.

**Supporting messages.**

| Message | Proof in the calendar |
|---|---|
| Four disciplines, one practice — tax, law, finance, HR | 06.10–13.10, the four-post sequence covering each |
| Uzbekistan-specific knowledge you cannot get from a global firm's regional page | November in full: ТМФЦ, СЭЗ, лицензирование, исламское финансирование |
| We can place this jurisdiction against the ones you already know | 19.11 ТМФЦ против AIFC и DIFC; December's global cases |
| The mechanism, not the marketing | The editorial rules in `social/README.md`: no hook, no bait, every slide a complete proposition |

**Message hierarchy per post.** Each topic answers, in order: why this is a
risk or an opportunity, how the mechanism works, what it looks like here,
what to do about it. The plan's "one topic, four cuts" is the same
hierarchy cut at different depths — LinkedIn takes all four steps, Twitter
takes the first sentence of step one.

**No differentiation claims in copy.** The brand refuses the register. The
differentiation is carried by the topic list and by the absence of hooks;
it is never asserted.

## 5. Content calendar

The topic calendar is in `PLAN.md` — twenty-six dated topics across three
months, verified internally consistent: eleven in October, eight in
November, seven in December; fourteen derived from published articles, nine
new, three drafted; the five reserve articles are correctly held out.
Dates match weekdays for 2026.

the dates by which text has to exist, which run ahead of the publication

| 14–20 Dec | Month-three review; decide on `/store` route | January | Javad | | not started |

**Every row is Javad's.** The framework asks for an owner per row because a
date without a name is a wish, and the answer here is that there is one
person. That settles who, and it sharpens what the schedule is actually
claiming: nine rows of production, one owner, thirteen weeks, starting from
a row that is already overdue. Two of the items cannot be delegated even in
principle — the firm facts, and which figures may be stated publicly. The
rest could be, if there were anyone to delegate to.

So the constraint is no longer ownership but capacity, and it moves to the
top of the risk list: a single-owner calendar has no slack in it. The place
that shows first is the 117 pieces of copy in § 6 — see the batching note
there, which is what makes the number survivable.

Three rules from the framework that the plan should adopt: leave roughly a
fifth of Twitter slots open for reactive material; batch text production by
month rather than by week; and work backwards from the publication date
rather than forwards from today — which is what the lead times above do and
what the plan, which lists only publication dates, does not. Writing four cuts of one topic in
one sitting is what makes the four cuts different rather than four
wrappers — which is the plan's own stated standard.

## 6. Assets required

**Per topic, must-have**: Instagram carousel (TOML plus six rendered cards),
Facebook post RU, Facebook post EN, Twitter post EN. LinkedIn long-form EN
on Tuesday topics only — thirteen of twenty-six.

That is 26 × 4 + 13 = **117 pieces of copy**, plus 26 carousels at six
cards each, plus roughly 10 Saturday notes. Against a plan whose stated
cost is "writing the text — once a month, for the whole month", this is the
number that decides whether the schedule is real. Nothing in the plan or
the repository estimates it.

The framework's own benchmark for a social post is one to two business days
from draft to scheduled. Taken per piece that gives a number with no
relation to a thirteen-week campaign, which is the point: **the batching is
load-bearing, not a preference.** One topic researched once and cut four
ways in a sitting is the only shape in which 117 pieces fits, and it is
also the shape the plan needs editorially — four cuts written apart become
four wrappers. If the batching slips to piece-by-piece production, the
schedule fails on arithmetic before it fails on quality.

**Covers**: none needed. All fourteen derived topics have article images in
`public/Articles Image/`; carousel covers generate from the header.

**Must-have infrastructure, not yet built** — see `social/README.md` § Not
done yet, and risk 1:

| Asset | State |
|---|---|
| Card renderer | Built (`render.py`, six slide types) |
| Rendered PNGs reaching the deployed site | Not built — output is gitignored, no CI render-and-commit workflow |
| Meta / LinkedIn / X publishing | Not built |
| LEAP setup document (referenced in Phase 0) | Not in this repository |

**Nice-to-have**: a `compare` card for 19.11 (the slide type exists and is
exercised only in `_specimen.toml`); a short pinned Instagram post
explaining what the account is, after the first six establish it.

## 7. Success metrics

`PLAN.md` § Measurement names the right metrics and sets no targets.
On a zero baseline targets are guesses, but a guess written down is
falsifiable and an absent one is not. Directional, to be replaced by real
numbers at the end of month one:

| KPI | Target by 22.12 | Tracked |
|---|---|---|
| Enquiries naming a post | ≥ 1 | Contact form and inbox, asked at intake |
| Topics published on schedule | 26 of 26 | This calendar |
| Instagram followers | 300–500 | Native |
| Instagram saves per post | Median above 2% of reach | Native — saves matter more than likes for this material |
| Link clicks to `/insights`, all platforms | 250+ | Site analytics by referrer |
| LinkedIn page: complete and 13 pieces live | Yes | Manual |

The plan's two editorial rules stand and are the point of measuring at all:
a topic below half the account's median drops out of rotation; a topic well
above it becomes a candidate for a full article. Both need a median, which
needs about eight posts — so the first real read is early November, not
October.

Reporting: monthly. Anything more frequent on a zero baseline is noise.

## 9. Risks

Risk 1 is still the one that can stop the campaign dead in October. Risk 6
is the one that is already happening.

**1. The automation Phase 2 depends on does not exist.** From 15 October
the plan publishes twice a week through an API. The repository has no
render-on-push workflow, keeps rendered cards out of git, and has no Meta,
LinkedIn or X publishing at all. Instagram will not accept bytes — a card
has to be live at a public URL before it can be published anywhere. This is
the single risk that can stop the campaign dead mid-October.
*Mitigation*: treat the pipeline as a Phase 0 deliverable with a hard date
of 12 October, and hold manual publishing in reserve through the end of
October. Manual is slower, not impossible — Phase 1 is manual by design.

**2. Ten Saturday slots are unassigned.** Saturday notes are Twitter-only
and topic-independent, so no line in the calendar produces them. They will
be written the morning they are due or not at all.
*Mitigation*: write six at a time alongside the monthly batch; they cost a
sentence each.

**3. Nine of twenty-six topics have no source and no draft**, weighted into
November and December, after the article bank thins. The plan anticipates
the ratio shift; it does not schedule the extra writing.
*Mitigation*: the five reserve articles exist for exactly this. Substitute
rather than miss a slot, and do it at the month boundary, not the week.

**4. Facebook costs double for the weakest channel** — a Russian and an
English post per topic, 52 pieces, on the channel with the oldest audience
and the least growth. *Mitigation*: review after month one. If English
Facebook underperforms Russian by a wide margin, drop it and let LinkedIn
carry English.

**5. The three December drafts are unchecked.** Dates and sums in
winklevoss, the 25% threshold and the FATF reference in ubo. A wrong figure
in a firm's first quarter is expensive in a way an empty week is not.
*Mitigation*: primary-source pass before 1 November, per the calendar. A
topic that fails the check gets replaced, not softened.

**6. Phase 0 is overdue, and its owner has no second.** As of 21 September
all three items in `PLAN.md` § Open before drafting starts are still open,
with ten days to the first post. Ownership is now settled — every row in
§ 5 is Javad's — which converts this from an unassigned risk into a
single-point-of-failure one: nothing in the calendar has a fallback, and an
illness or a client week in October takes the schedule with it. One of them, Facebook Page
Publishing Authorization, takes days to clear and blocks API publishing
entirely; it has to be checked this week or Facebook is manual through
October whatever the pipeline does. The plan said to find out now. Nothing
records who is finding out, and that is why the date slipped rather than
being missed for any substantive reason.
*Mitigation*: the two items that genuinely cannot be delegated are the
firm facts and which figures may be stated publicly. Everything else —
the PPA check, the Meta setup, the rendering — is checking and could be
handed over the moment there is a second pair of hands. Until then, run the
month's batch early rather than on the date, so that a lost week costs
slack instead of a slot.
